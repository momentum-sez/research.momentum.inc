Intelligent Assets

Assets that Carry Their Own Rules, Compose Across Jurisdictions, and Act Autonomously

Author: Raeez Lorgat

Abstract. Standard valuation theory reduces every tradeable instrument to a probability-weighted discounted future cash flow: a claim. An intelligent asset combines that claim with its full lifecycle program. Its state carries the rules, proofs, and programs that govern its actions and movement between jurisdictions, with machine-checkable evidence for each step.

An intelligent asset has six parts: a genesis record, a claim program encoding the instrument’s contingent terms, a family of lifecycle programs, a portfolio of sovereign-signed attestations, an append-only receipt chain, and a persistent store. A lifecycle program can move the claim only through the authority gates the claim declares. The genesis record fixes an invariant set and a single amendment gate outside the amendable surface. No chain of runtime-admitted amendments can rewrite either, so an asset cannot rewrite its own mandate. A typed compliance function admits actions under three structural guarantees: a failing required-domain verdict is final and cannot be offset by another domain’s evidence, unattested domains block, and a domain change flags exactly its dependents for re-evaluation. The claim program and verdict also determine who may hold the asset, which carried evidence a receiving jurisdiction may rely on, and what the asset is worth. Matching, settlement, risk reservation, dispute, and recovery over these quantities remain an open specification.

The Smart Asset Virtual Machine is deterministic: it admits an action only on a passing pre-flight verdict and appends a receipt to every execution. Delegated artificial-intelligence programs may propose actions but cannot approve them. Their authority is the overlap between compliance-passing and delegated actions, subject to monetary caps and delegation revocable at any moment. Human judgment enters at declared decision points under signed authorization. A supervisory halt order outranks every private delegation.

The taxonomy spans twelve classes, from payments operators, investment funds, sukuk, and intellectual-property indices through treasuries, physical infrastructure, consented data, stewarded natural systems, trade receivables, and commodities in transit. An asset’s attested compliance history reduces the compliance-verification component of due diligence to replay of the attested chain. Where a shortage of capital limits a trade corridor, this lower verification cost lets volume grow. Eight worked lifecycles illustrate the construction under a stated trust model and explicit open problems. Evaluator correctness is a scoped trust assumption, legal force depends on external instruments, coalition formation remains political, and scenario numbers are declared parameters.

1. What Is Broken

A three-person cross-border payments company operates in the United States, the United Kingdom, Singapore, the United Arab Emirates, and Nigeria. Compliance consumes a large fraction of its revenue: outside counsel in five jurisdictions, one full-time hire, a licensing stack, and the same know-your-customer question answered five times. Adding a sixth jurisdiction would cost half again as much, so the company stops expanding and leaves customers it could serve unreached.

A stewardship trust holds one hundred thousand hectares of the Amazon rainforest and issues carbon-biodiversity credits under a voluntary-market methodology. Three national environmental agencies, representatives of an indigenous council recognized under national constitutions, and a scientific advisory board establish it by compact. Every sale must address additionality (would the forest have stood without this project), permanence (will it stand for the next hundred years), and leakage (does protecting this tract displace clearing elsewhere). The trust spends two years registering one vintage.

A Saudi electronics-components exporter ships to a Shenzhen-based manufacturer and receives payment forty-five to sixty days later. The letter-of-credit cycle requires know-your-customer checks on the buyer and continuous screening against U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control, European Union, and United Nations sanctions lists. Export-control classification, bank-intermediated foreign-exchange hedging, and manual customs and bill-of-lading reconciliation add further work. Pension funds, insurers, and sovereign wealth vehicles hold trillions of dollars in short-duration credit but cannot buy the receivable. The paper is unrated, verifying its compliance chain requires manual re-execution, and per-receivable due-diligence cost exceeds the financing spread. Bilateral trade between the Arabian peninsula and China is on the order of four hundred billion dollars a year. Some growth that exporters could ship and buyers could absorb exceeds the receivables-financing cycle’s capacity. Section 18.2 leaves the size of this capital-gated fraction to corridor-level analysis.

The company, the trust, and the receivable face the same problem: each action must satisfy rules administered across institutional boundaries.

Different authorities enforce these rules under different frameworks and in different jurisdictions. The rules carry legal force, and violating them is criminal, yet they do not travel with the institutional object. Each cross-jurisdictional action rebuilds the compliance context the last jurisdiction established. Adding a jurisdiction multiplies existing obligations, while attestations remain confined to the institutions that produced them. Parties reconstruct the composition of rules manually for each transaction.

Determining what may be done, by whom, under which rules, and in which jurisdictions still takes manual work measured in months. Large institutions spend on the order of ten to twenty per cent of operating cost on compliance departments. Smaller or less established structures face exclusions: the three-person company cannot reach its fifth market, the exporter’s financing cycle caps volume despite demand, and the stewardship structure cannot connect an indigenous council’s consent to a corporate buyer’s climate report.

Policy changes have increased these costs. Sanctions fragmentation since 2022 has produced cascading secondary-sanctions designations. For many affected cross-border flows, reconciling compliance costs more than the flows’ economic value. De-risking has halved some small jurisdictions’ correspondent-banking base within a decade and reduced or removed much of the developing world’s correspondent access to dollar and euro systems. The BIS Committee on Payments and Market Infrastructures’ correspondent-banking review and the Financial Stability Board’s data reports document this contraction. Asymmetric adoption of the OECD’s Pillar-2 framework and Schrems-II-class data-transfer volatility further raise cross-border operating costs, making such operation uneconomic for firms unable to absorb them.

An object that addresses these costs must carry its rules in its own state and make their mechanical fragment evaluable in microseconds. Named evaluators must attest it under named rule sets. Its rules must compose algebraically across jurisdictions, with defined behavior at edge cases. Programs must operate under scoped, revocable delegations, while sovereigns retain an operational control surface. The primitive must also state its limits.

The intelligent asset defined here supplies these properties under the assumptions and limitations stated below.

2. What Changes

An intelligent asset may be a fund, a bond, a treasury pool, a physical facility, a consented corpus, or a stewardship trust. It carries six things throughout its lifecycle: its creation record, the claim program encoding its terms, the programs operating within an envelope authorized at founding, sovereign attestations establishing current compliance state, an append-only history of actions to or by it, and a store that persists between executions.

Before an action, a compliance function evaluates it against every required regulatory domain using attestations from every jurisdiction in whose territory the object operates. When applicability disagrees across jurisdictions, the composition algebra returns a structured obstruction and preserves the disagreement. A passing verdict lets the action execute, its effects settle, and a new receipt join the history. A failing verdict halts the action and records the cause.

Compiled compliance gates compute deterministically from the attestation portfolio and the action’s required domains. Rule evaluators compiled from the companion rule language Lex produce verdicts with explicit provenance and typed discretion holes where mechanical computation must stop. Delegated proposal programs operate under signed principal delegations. Each delegation specifies tools, resources, limits, and which discretion holes the program may attempt to fill or must escalate to principals. These programs have read-only access to the compliance tensor and submit proposed actions to the compliance gate for admission or refusal. Their rationales join the receipt chain, which supplies the audit record.

Ownership changes extend the receipt chain. A sovereign revocation returns the cells downstream of the attestation to pending and triggers the propagation graph to recompute the re-evaluation set. The object then freezes or degrades its capabilities until fresh attestations arrive. When it adds or retires a harbor, a bilaterally-agreed corridor specifies which attestations carry through under mutual recognition and which require fresh evaluation. The transition is atomic under the fail-stop and finality-certificate assumptions in the companion protocol papers. Outside those assumptions, it returns a typed obstruction or enters a bounded-blocking compensation path.

The same algebra binds autonomy to compliance state for a bond, a data center, or a rainforest. Cross-jurisdictional compliance becomes proof verification plus fresh evaluation of the domains a corridor names. This change makes previously uneconomic instruments economic and lets institutional forms retain their structure across borders.

The legal force of attestations still requires political adoption. Legal regimes may reference the infrastructure, but the architecture alone creates no legal force. Section 17 collects the preconditions stated throughout the paper.

Intelligent assets connect issuance and governance to clearing and settlement. The issuance and governance layer is the sovereign jurisdiction network described in the companion paper of that name. It hosts the multi-harbored institution issuing the asset, evaluates the entity’s compliance state, and supplies the corridor parameters (R, \mu, \gamma) of Definition 4.5 for carried evidence. The clearing and settlement layer consumes the composed admissibility envelope, corridor parameters, claim program, and lifecycle programs as typed inputs. It also needs the admissible holder set, reliance class, and clearing price constructed in Definitions 4.6 to 4.8. Matching, settlement, risk reservation, dispute, and recovery over them remain the open specification of §17.4. This paper defines the typed object class connecting the layers. The sovereign-jurisdiction-network paper defines the substrate on which it operates.

3. Eight Worked Lifecycles

Eight lifecycles show what the formal object must support, from genesis to a consequential event. Three develop the situations in §1. The others extend the construction to funds, treasuries, data centers, consented corpora, and cargo. Every cost, headcount, timeline, spread, and other number in these lifecycles is a declared scenario parameter, not an empirical claim. A live deployment would have to measure each one. The formalism that follows defines the primitives used here.

3.1 A cross-border payments company (Class I, financial operator)

Three founders based in Lagos, London, and Singapore incorporate a payments company in Delaware with operating subsidiaries in the United Kingdom, the United Arab Emirates, Singapore, and Nigeria. They intend to move remittances and small-business working capital across five corridors. The company registers as an intelligent asset with five harbors (U.S., U.K., U.A.E., Singapore, Nigeria) and a three-signature authoring quorum over the founders’ keys. Its initial programs include compiled anti-money-laundering, sanctions, and consumer-protection gates, plus a rule evaluator compiled from its published terms of service. Foreign-exchange reporting appears as methodology fields inside the canonical Payments, Banking, and Tax domains. A narrowly delegated proposal program may read the tensor, propose customer onboarding, draft disclosures, and escalate novel situations to the founders.

For three months the program proposes customer onboardings. Each proposal is gated: the sanctions cell of the destination harbor must be Compliant, the anti-money-laundering cell in the source harbor must be Compliant, and the corridor’s foreign-exchange reporting requirement must be satisfied inside its canonical domain. When a proposal passes, the customer is onboarded, the receipt carries the program’s rationale, the counterparty identity-check provider’s attestation, and a reference to the rule version under which the evaluation ran. When it fails, the receipt records the blocking domain and the program drafts a customer-facing explanation.

In month five, the company adds Brazil as a sixth harbor. The Brazilian central bank’s designated evaluator evaluates the corridor between Brazil and the existing harbors. Consumer-protection and foreign-exchange reporting require fresh evaluation because their rule sets differ. Sanctions is re-evaluated locally under the general-purpose corridor rule, and remaining domains carry through only where recognition and grade maps are defined. Fresh attestations issue in two weeks, after which the company begins operating in Brazil.

In month eleven, a sanctions update adds a new designated party. The sanctions cells across harbors transition to Pending. Propagation flags customer-onboarding decisions that referenced the affected party as stale. Outstanding transactions to or from the affected party are blocked by the algebra before any manual review. The receipt chain records every block, its cause, and the rule version under which the block was decided.

In the scenario, the company’s compliance headcount over the first year is one, the compliance cost is one hundred twenty thousand dollars, and the company adds a seventh jurisdiction in month fifteen.

3.2 A cross-border private-equity fund (Class II, collective investment)

A private-equity fund with two billion dollars of committed capital restructures as an intelligent asset. Its genesis document names four harbors (Cayman, Delaware, Luxembourg, Singapore) and a four-signature authoring quorum of the investment committee. Its programs include Level-1 securities, custody, tax, and sanctions gates across each harbor, and a Level-1 net-asset-value computation keyed to monthly close. A Level-2 rebalancing program reads the portfolio, computes target allocations under the mandate, and proposes counterparty trades. Trades above a per-trade monetary cap go to the investment committee. A separate Level-2 program evaluates harbor transitions and may propose, but not execute, corridor operations when regulatory terms shift.

Limited partners receive a read-only receipt-chain view scoped to their partnership interest in place of the quarterly letter. Reconciliation hours per investment decision fall by sixty per cent. Custodian, fund-administrator, and prime-brokerage attestations enter through the kernel’s watcher-attestation interface, making the receipt chain the canonical reconciliation record. The administrator still computes and signs net asset value. Its sign-off enters the tax and custody cells as a watcher-attestation instead of an email to the general partner.

In year two, Luxembourg weakens certain alternative-investment-fund-manager disclosures in a way incompatible with the fund’s stated investor-protection posture. The board would call this regulatory capture. The harbor-transition evaluation program identifies preferable terms in Ireland, evaluates the corridor, and proposes adding Ireland and retiring Luxembourg. It escalates the proposal with a draft limited-partner communication to the investment committee. After the committee signs, Ireland evaluates the re-evaluation set (four domains with differing rule sets), the remaining domains carry through, entry attestations issue, and the transition commits. The receipt chain records every step, including the committee’s tax counsel’s signed analysis as a discretion-hole fill.

In the scenario, the harbor transition takes two weeks rather than nine months. The corridor is already negotiated, the re-evaluation set is narrow, and limited partners consent through a signed partnership-agreement amendment drafted by the program and reviewed by the committee. Human counsel handles the tax implications, including the §367 analysis of the outbound deemed exchange for U.S. feeders, and signs a principal-carried authorization. The receipt chain carries counsel’s signature alongside the transition.

3.3 A multi-jurisdictional treasury (Class VII, working-capital operator)

The in-house bank of a Fortune-500 multinational restructures as an intelligent asset. Its genesis document names eight harbors (the jurisdictions in which the group maintains functional banking relationships and material cash balances), a four-signature principal quorum (treasurer, group tax head, chief financial officer, chief accounting officer), and a program set dominated by Level-2 programs: cash-pooling and netting, transfer pricing, Pillar-2 top-up-tax calculation, country-by-country reporting, and Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act / Common Reporting Standard reporting. Level-1 gates enforce per-harbor sanctions, anti-money-laundering, and per-transaction monetary caps.

The transfer-pricing program reads intercompany loan rates, trade-pricing attestations signed by operating subsidiaries’ local-file signers, and the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development’s Multilateral Instrument-treaty lookups. Each quarter it drafts a master-file summary. The group tax head signs it as a principal-carried authorization, which enters the tax cells of every harbor as attestations. The Pillar-2 program drafts Global Anti-Base-Erosion top-up-tax computations from signed inputs and identifies possible safe-harbor opportunities: Transitional Country-by-Country Reporting Safe Harbor elections and Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax elections. It escalates elections, treaty positions, and uncertain classifications to the treasurer and group tax head as typed discretion holes. Formal cross-harbor GloBE/treaty/tax-fiber composition remains an open obligation rather than a solved meet operation.

The system produces the country-by-country-reporting receipt continuously rather than annually. To test the report, the external auditor re-executes the receipt chain’s computations against the underlying attestations, replacing a three-week reconciliation.

The intelligent-asset layer connects to the existing enterprise-resource-planning and treasury stack. Finance-document events enter the kernel’s oracle surface as signed watcher-attestations. Kernel-emitted attestations return to the general ledger as typed journal entries recognized by its reconciliation routine. The incumbent systems remain in place.

In the scenario, the treasury’s reconciliation headcount falls from forty-three to fourteen over eighteen months. The annual cost of compliance and reporting falls from eighteen million dollars to five million, with the remainder concentrated in the residual human judgments the discretion-hole mechanism surfaces.

3.4 A hyperscale data-center operator (Class VIII, physical infrastructure)

A data-center operator restructures as an intelligent asset, with each site a sub-asset under the parent entity. Each site’s genesis document names three harbors: the physical jurisdiction of the land, the operating-entity jurisdiction (often a separate-purpose vehicle elsewhere), and the grid-interconnect market operator’s authority. Level-1 gates cover environmental-impact reporting within Licensing and Trade, labor-law compliance within Employment, and U.S. Export Administration Regulations / European Union dual-use classifications within Trade and Sanctions. Level-2 sensor-fusion programs consume telemetry under a Byzantine quorum across four independent sensor-vendor feeds before any attestation is signed. An operations program connects to the incumbent facility-management stack through typed tool interfaces.

When a tenant proposes a workload, the operations program reads its declared type and computes the export-control classification under the current rule version. It identifies the cells that must pass: Sanctions for tenant and end-customer, Trade/Sanctions for workload classification, Licensing/Trade for additional power draw, and Employment for on-site service. It then proposes an attestation bundle. Approval is automatic when every cell is Compliant and the workload lies within the tenant’s standing delegation. A novel classification requires a discretion-hole fill by the export-control officer.

Continuous power-usage-effectiveness attestation, signed by the sensor-fusion program under a quorum of four vendors, feeds the environmental-impact methodology field inside the relevant Licensing and Trade cells. The site’s annual environmental audit, previously a six-figure exercise per site, becomes a verifying re-execution against the receipt chain. Insurance premium negotiations with the business-interruption insurer reference the receipt-chain quality; any premium change remains an underwriting outcome.

When a Brazilian site’s grid operator declares a demand-response event, the grid-interconnect harbor’s evaluator signs an attestation. The operations program proposes the prescribed load reduction, and the Level-1 gate checks operational limits. The action executes within the grid operator’s deadline. The demand-response payment then settles through the receipt chain as a cross-referenced attestation with the grid operator.

The operator’s per-site compliance cost falls from eight million to one million dollars within eighteen months. It opens two additional sites in the window during which its prior cost structure would have admitted none.

3.5 A consented genomic research corpus (Class IX, consent-governed scientific asset)

A genomic research consortium restructures its corpus as an intelligent asset in which each participant is a first-class principal. Its genesis document names the jurisdictional harbors (the participating countries’ research-ethics regimes), the institutional authoring principal (a consortium board), and a Merkle-rooted participant-principal tree: each of two-point-four million participants is represented as a leaf, with individual revocation rights over their own data, aggregated through the root into consortium-level operations.

Consent is a structured cell. It carries tiers (broad research vs specific study), purposes (research, commercial, statistical-training, internal-QA), geographic scopes (must remain in European Economic Area / may transfer under adequacy decisions / may not enter the United States), and temporal decay (valid for five years, renewing on re-contact). Every proposed use is gated: the requesting researcher’s Institutional Review Board approval enters as a discretion-hole fill by the relevant ethics board, the composition of consent cells across the participants whose data the use touches must meet the use’s requirements, and the data-privacy cells across the harbors must be Compliant or Exempt.

When a participant withdraws consent, the withdrawal event enters as an oracle attestation signed by the participant (or by the consortium’s withdrawal-officer on the participant’s instruction). Propagation extends through the regulatory-domain graph and through a derivative-use graph: datasets referencing the withdrawn records are flagged stale; already-licensed derivatives incur notification obligations; statistical artifacts containing the participant’s data are flagged for refresh at the next cycle. The withdrawal is a monotonically-propagating state transition with explicit semantics.

A pre-committed formula splits commercial-use royalties among institutions, participants, the scientific-advisory-board, and a research-infrastructure fund. Receipt-chain-anchored transfers execute the split. Nagoya-Protocol benefit-sharing has a distinct evaluator and attestation surface where it applies to genetic resources from biodiversity-rich jurisdictions.

The consortium remains the institution participants trust, with cryptography supporting it. Participants can mechanically check their rights and audit their exercise. Legal enforceability remains subject to the recognition preconditions of §17.1.

3.6 A stewardship trust for the Amazon rainforest (Class X, natural-system steward)

A stewardship trust for one hundred thousand hectares of Amazon rainforest restructures as an intelligent asset under a multi-sovereign compact. Its harbors span Brazil, Peru, Colombia, and an indigenous council recognized under the three nations’ constitutional and international obligations. Recognition of the council is a precondition that the architecture cannot supply. The authoring principal is a six-signature quorum: three national environmental agencies, two indigenous-council representatives, and a scientific advisory board. Two council representatives are a structural minimum rather than a maximum, and the council’s delegation rules govern their selection. Level-1 gates cover environmental-impact reporting, carbon-accounting methodology, and indigenous-rights compliance within Licensing, Trade, ConsumerProtection, Arbitration, DataPrivacy, and Ip, plus sanctions screening on buyers. Level-2 programs monitor satellite imagery, ground sensors, auditor attestations, and community reports. They also handle credit issuance, buyer negotiation, and remediation response.

Free, prior, and informed consent requires a structured process beyond the signature event. Every consequential action requiring community consent enters a deliberation window under the council’s own governance rules. This includes credit-vintage issuance, a new buyer jurisdiction, a methodology change, and response to a major oracle event. The council deliberates in its working language with the information it requires, and consent is captured only when the window closes. Withdrawal remains available until the vintage closes. The council’s constitution governs key custody: threshold signing, rotation, recovery, and non-digital fallback paths when digital infrastructure fails.

A cross-asset buffer pool enforces permanence by reserving a share of every vintage against reversals. The reserve composes across trusts using the same methodology, whose risk model determines its size. Adjacent-tract monitoring and methodology-level tracts account for leakage. The trust’s tensor belongs to a methodology-level graph whose nodes share observations used to price regional displacement. At vintage registration, the methodology’s evaluator attests additionality under its rule set. The trust itself does not make that determination.

Credit sales are inter-asset negotiations. The buyer, itself an intelligent asset representing a corporate treasury or compliance pool, and the trust each evaluate their tensor. Under the corridor finality assumptions, passing verdicts on both sides allow settlement and record the buyer’s claim through both receipt chains. A buyer’s jurisdiction may have signed on to Article 6 of the Paris Agreement and require a corresponding adjustment against the host jurisdiction’s nationally-determined contribution. The host jurisdiction’s designated authority then signs a distinct corresponding-adjustment attestation.

A deforestation event detected by the monitoring program transitions the relevant Licensing/Trade methodology field to NonCompliant. Propagation invalidates downstream methodology fields for carbon accounting and biodiversity claims. Active vintages enter the buffer-pool replacement protocol: affected tonnes are replaced from the buffer, the buyer’s claim is made whole from the buffer pool rather than clawed back, the revocation-and-replacement mechanics flow through the receipt chain. The scientific advisory board receives a disclosure. The indigenous council, on its own deliberation, decides whether to invoke an emergency-response clause. Its decision is a discretion-hole fill.

The trust is the rainforest’s credit-issuing steward. The physical territory and national environmental sovereignty remain fixed. When regime conditions change, the trust may change its operating harbor only with the receiving jurisdiction’s willingness to engage.

3.7 A trade-receivables pool across the Arabian-Chinese corridor

Consider a Saudi electronics-components exporter selling to a Shenzhen-based manufacturer. Before intelligent-asset infrastructure, shipment-to-settlement takes forty-five to sixty days. Buyer know-your-customer takes two weeks, while sanctions screening continuously checks changing U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control, European Union, and United Nations lists. Export-control classification takes five to seven business days under the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia’s General Authority for Foreign Trade and the receiving jurisdiction’s import-control regime. A United States dollar-renminbi hedge adds bank-intermediation spreads. Customs, bill-of-lading, and carrier attestations arrive through uncoordinated channels for manual reconciliation. Pension funds, insurers, and sovereign wealth vehicles mostly cannot finance these receivables: the paper is unrated, its compliance chain requires manual re-execution, and due-diligence cost exceeds the financing spread. Receivables remain on exporters’ balance sheets or receive bank financing at three-to-four-per-cent spreads. In a capital-gated Arabian-Chinese corridor, working-capital velocity can bind despite demand. Turning cash in days instead of months may let exporters ship more, while bounded-cost payment extensions may let buyers order more.

Each receivable becomes an intelligent asset at origination. Its genesis names four harbors: Saudi Arabia as the exporter’s jurisdiction, with corridors through the Abu Dhabi Global Market’s financial-services regime; the People’s Republic of China as the buyer’s jurisdiction, via Shanghai or Shenzhen; Abu Dhabi Global Market or the Dubai International Financial Centre as intermediary, each with established bilateral corridors; and Hong Kong as settlement harbor under its cross-boundary arrangements. A threshold-signature over the exporter’s treasurer and the buyer’s enterprise-resource-planning system’s watcher-attestation forms the authoring principal. Initial attestations load immediately. The buyer’s Chinese correspondent bank signs know-your-customer evidence, the Abu Dhabi Global Market’s designated evaluator signs sanctions clearance, and the General Authority for Foreign Trade’s designated evaluator signs export-control classification. The maritime carrier signs shipment evidence chained to the bill-of-lading digest. Parallel production under existing legal infrastructure and algebraic composition let every required domain pass within hours rather than weeks of manual reconciliation.

A pool of these receivables, such as the next quarter’s Saudi-Shenzhen electronics-components flow, is another intelligent asset. Its published methodology governs aggregation, concentration limits, and expected-loss computation. Institutional investors acquire tranches through Op’s signed commitment protocol, locking capital against the tranche and receivables against its coverage. Under the protocol’s finality assumptions, settlement commits on shipment-attestation confirmation or compensates on failure. The investor’s risk team can re-execute the pool’s compliance state as a receipt-chain projection. Credit-rating agencies assign the rating by running their own evaluator against the chain instead of consuming an issuer’s self-report. Spreads compress under a financing model in which verification cost is the binding component.

Algebraic verifiability removes one reason allocators cannot buy trade paper. The pool becomes investable only when the conditions of §18.1 also hold, and each allocator still makes its own investment decision.

Working-capital velocity gives a conditional trade-volume calculation. A receivable turning in two days rather than forty-five can finance twenty times as many shipments per year from the same capital before other constraints bind. Access to institutional financing can expand the pool beyond bank credit, and lower compliance friction can admit exporters previously excluded by cycle cost. Whether financing ceases to be the binding constraint depends on ports, manufacturing throughput, shipping availability, demand, and policy. The mechanism applies to other corridors only where the cost of reconciling bilateral compliance constrains trade more than underlying commercial demand.

The architecture defines when per-transaction compliance composition can move from manual reconciliation to algebraic verification. This addresses a receivables-financing bottleneck where trade growth is capital-gated. Demand and the political preconditions of bilateral trade, including recognition, visa regimes, and physical logistics, remain external. U.S., E.U., and Chinese export-control and sanctions regimes continue to bind.

Incumbent rails continue to carry the exporter’s letter-of-credit cycle through SWIFT MT700 issuance and MT710 advising where the buyer’s bank requires them. Under UCP 600 Article 14’s documentary-examination standard, attestation-signed carrier, surveyor, and customs documents enter as electronic equivalents under eUCP version 2.1 or as Bolero electronic bills of lading. Documentary discrepancies occur in twenty-to-thirty per cent of presentations in this scenario. The Saudi General Authority for Foreign Trade’s HS-code attestation may differ from the Chinese General Administration of Customs’ HS-code attestation, or the bill-of-lading’s shipment date from the surveyor’s quality-certificate date. These discrepancies trigger a structured discretion hole under UCP 600 Article 16, allowing reconciliation to continue. The resolver, typically the applicant’s bank’s documentary team, signs a principal-carried authorization with a named waiver whose substance the receipt chain records. Documentary-discrepancy resolution thus retains its role within a machine-auditable protocol. The dollar leg settles through SWIFT gpi when the parties choose it, or through regulated digital cash when both admit it. The renminbi leg settles through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System when one party prefers it. Treasury platforms, supply-chain-finance platforms, and bank-internal portals can originate into the intelligent-asset pool, which provides programmable compliance and can issue to their investor channels where the paper is admissible.

3.8 A commodity cargo across the Persian Gulf-East Asia corridor

A merchant trading house loads a very-large crude carrier at Ras Tanura bound for Ningbo with two million barrels of Arabian Light under a charter party and an open-account sales agreement. The Class XII cargo carries the attestations already issued by sovereign and non-sovereign parties as one algebraic object in place of a manual file. Its harbor set comprises the authorities in the physical-custody graph. Saudi Arabia’s General Authority for Foreign Trade signs export clearance, the flag-state maritime authority signs shipping registration, China’s General Administration of Customs signs import clearance, and Ningbo Port Authority signs discharge-port evidence. Non-sovereign evaluators include SGS or Bureau Veritas for cargo quality (metal content, moisture, chemistry, bill of quantities) and Lloyd’s Register of Shipping for vessel class. The tensor composes Sanctions, Trade, KYC, Custody, Insurance, and Arbitration. Export control, origin declaration, cargo quality, counterparty identity, and chain-of-custody integrity are methodology fields within those canonical domains.

The cargo’s dispute-resolution forum is named at genesis and its procedural rules are attested. Depending on the contract, the forum is arbitration under the London Maritime Arbitrators Association or the rules of GAFTA, FOSFA, LME, LCIA, ICC, or SIAC, or High Court jurisdiction in London or New York. A process-typed discretion hole records the resolution as a principal-carried authorization under the named arbitral institution’s process type. Possession risk has a separate escalation class and insurance-binder attestation conditions from payment risk. It covers vessel arrest in the Strait of Hormuz, cargo detention at discharge, and war-risk-zone diversion.

A secondary-sanctions event propagates along a chain-of-custody graph orthogonal to the regulatory-domain graph of §7. Designating a downstream counterparty changes its sanctions cell. The propagation engine then identifies every cargo that has crossed that party’s custody and flags its compliance state for re-evaluation before any downstream transfer clears. This graph formalizes secondary-sanctions propagation in merchant trading and distinguishes Class XII from Class XI’s purely-financial receivables. The voyage’s freight derivative, forward-freight agreement, Brent or West Texas Intermediate futures hedge, or time-spread options position is a paired intelligent asset. It composes with the cargo under Op’s multi-asset signed commitment. Under finality assumptions, unwinding the physical leg unwinds the hedge through the same settlement evidence.

For the merchant, a propagation-engine query and bounded set of gated actions replace seventy-two hours of manual reconciliation after a secondary-sanctions event. Compliance-reconciliation overhead falls, and the hedge and physical leg compose through settlement evidence instead of desk-side reconciliation. The cargo’s documentation forms a single algebraic object that the merchant’s auditor, regulator, and buyer can each re-execute independently.

The lifecycles require the same core operations: scoped admission, authorized state changes, and a verifiable history. The next section defines the record that supports them.

4. The Object

An intelligent asset is a typed record whose fields supply the interfaces used by the other fields and the runtime. Throughout this section and §15, it is a programmatic contingent claim. Its claim program encodes the instrument’s contingent claim under the probability-weighted discounted-cash-flow reduction. A coupled lifecycle program family specifies the typed transitions its runtime may execute. Compliance, attestation, and receipt mechanisms make the claim and its lifecycle clearable across jurisdictions.

Definition 4.1 (Intelligent asset). An intelligent asset is a six-tuple

A = (g, \Phi, \Lambda, \pi, \sigma, s)

whose fields are:

  • Genesis document g \in \mathrm{Genesis}, the immutable record of creation, content-addressed by digest h_g. The genesis names the asset class (its placement in the contingent-claim taxonomy of §15), the institutional-identity pointer (to a multi-harbored institution whose harbor set is time-varying and lives outside g), the authoring principal (a public-key identifier or a threshold-signature scheme over multiple keys), the initial digests of \Phi and \Lambda, the initial storage schema, and the initial attestation-portfolio specification. Amendments to the harbor set, the program set, and, under specified governance rules, the authoring principal itself, are not edits to g; they are signed amendment records appended to \sigma and resolved dynamically when the asset is read. Two further genesis fields bound that amendable surface from outside it: an invariant set \mathcal{I} of decidable predicates on the amendable record, the triple of claim program, program family, and authoring principal that amendment records leave resolved, and an amendment gate \theta_\ast, the single authority gate (Definition 4.9) wrapping every amendment-kind transition. Neither \mathcal{I} nor \theta_\ast lies in the range of any amendment record, and both are fixed by h_g: “immutability of g” is the immutability of \mathcal{I} and \theta_\ast, while time-varying state is the province of the other fields and the institutional pointer. The construction, and the invariance it buys, close this section (Proposition 4.1).

  • Claim program \Phi \in \mathrm{ClaimProgram}, the term-level encoding of the instrument’s contingent claim under the probability-weighted discounted-cash-flow reduction. \mathrm{ClaimProgram} is the contract-combinator grammar of Peyton Jones and Eber (2003), descending from their 2000 paper with Seward, with terms c ::= \mathbf{zero} \mid \mathbf{one}(k) \mid \mathbf{give}(c) \mid \mathbf{and}(c_1, c_2) \mid \mathbf{or}(c_1, c_2) \mid \mathbf{cond}(o, c_1, c_2) \mid \mathbf{scale}(o, c) \mid \mathbf{when}(o, c) \mid \mathbf{anytime}(o, c) \mid \mathbf{until}(o, c), extended with the two constructors this architecture requires, \mathbf{gate}(\theta, c) and \mathbf{hole}(\eta, c), where k ranges over currencies, o over observables the asset’s oracle surface signs, \theta over the authority gates of Definition 4.9, and \eta over the typed discretion holes of Definition 11.1. The denotation \mathcal{V}(c) is the value process the combinator semantics assigns under a model for the observables. On the two added constructors it reads the asset’s own state: \mathcal{V}(\mathbf{gate}(\theta, c)) is \mathcal{V}(c) on the branch where \theta admits the transition attempted there (Definition 4.9: a passing §6 verdict on that action over \theta’s domain scope, together with a PCAuth under \theta’s process type) and the zero process otherwise, and \mathcal{V}(\mathbf{hole}(\eta, c)) is \mathcal{V}(c) on the branch where \sigma carries a PCAuth valid for \eta under Definition 11.2 and the zero process otherwise. So \Phi specifies when cash flows, in what amount, to which counterparty, under what authority gates, on default what happens, on dispute what happens, and on amendment what happens, and a lifecycle transition can be attempted at a lifecycle state only where \Phi’s gate and hole constructors leave a live branch there, which makes the coupling stated below a computation on \Phi’s denotation rather than a stipulation about \Lambda; Definition 9.3 then decides admission on the domains the handle requires. \Phi is the financial reading of the asset: the admissible holder set, the reliance class, and the clearing price are read off \mathcal{V}(\Phi) and the compliance function in Definitions 4.6 to 4.8. Concrete instances of \Phi correspond to claim classes in §15: a BondClaim \Phi encodes coupon schedule + face + covenants + default waterfall; a SukukClaim \Phi encodes Ijara/Murabaha/Musharaka/Mudaraba structure under Sharia certification; a ParametricInsuranceClaim \Phi encodes trigger event + payout schedule + reinsurer reserve; a RwaClaim \Phi encodes off-chain custody binding + receivable schedule. \Phi is content-addressed by digest h_\Phi recorded in g.

  • Lifecycle program family \Lambda, a finite set of typed program handles authorized by the genesis document. Each handle is a quadruple (h_p, \tau_\mathrm{in}, \tau_\mathrm{out}, D_\mathrm{req}) where h_p is the content-digest of the program artifact, \tau_\mathrm{in} and \tau_\mathrm{out} are input and output type signatures, and D_\mathrm{req} \subseteq \mathcal{D} is the statically declared set of compliance domains the program’s invocation requires. Each \lambda_i \in \Lambda admits a typed state transition in \Phi’s lifecycle (issuance, attestation, transfer, partial settlement, dispute, recovery, revocation, amendment, termination); the typed authority gates and discretion holes of \Phi govern which \lambda_i is admissible at each lifecycle state. Artifacts include Level-1 compiled compliance gates, Op-bytecode programs compiled from Lex rules (under the companion Op paper’s admissible-compilation theorem), and Level-2 delegated proposal-program configurations (see §10). Additions to \Lambda are signed amendments recorded in \sigma.

  • Attestation portfolio \pi \subseteq \mathrm{Att}, the set of currently valid sovereign attestations. The runtime maintains \pi under the invariant: for every \alpha \in \pi, the attestation’s validity window includes the current time and the attestation has not been revoked. Expired or revoked attestations are removed eagerly.

  • Receipt chain \sigma = (r_0, r_1, \ldots, r_n), a hash-linked sequence of execution receipts. r_0 is the genesis receipt; every r_{i+1} carries the digest of r_i in its parent field. Appends are serialized per asset by a compare-and-swap against the current chain head. On integrity failure (parent-digest mismatch, signature invalid), the runtime transitions the asset to a fault state and emits a runtime-signed fault receipt.

  • Persistent store s, a typed key-value map under a schema declared in g, surviving between program executions.

The pair (\Phi, \Lambda) couples the financial program \Phi to the operational programs \Lambda through \Phi’s typed authority gates and discretion holes. Without \Lambda, the asset is a record with signed metadata. With \Lambda, it observes external events, applies the compliance gate, and acts within its authorized lifecycle envelope. Actions outside that envelope require a signed amendment, which is not autonomous. The coupling between \Phi and \Lambda bounds a single transition: \Lambda may transition \Phi only through the typed-discretion-hole and authority-gate structure \Phi declares. Mandate invariance must hold over every chain of transitions, including amendments admitted by \Lambda. The one-step coupling alone cannot establish it. Proposition 4.1 proves the chain invariant from the invariant set and amendment gate fixed in g.

The terminal transition. Completion needs an explicit record. Otherwise, a matured bond, fully settled receivable, or dissolved company becomes permanently Pending as attestations expire. Its unsigned final record, which a court may need, cannot distinguish completion from abandonment. Termination is therefore a separate lifecycle kind, admitted only through a declared authority gate in \Phi and never inferred from attestation decay. A well-formed terminal transition discharges four obligations. Extinguishment: \Phi’s outstanding obligations are settled, discharged, or assigned to a named successor claim, with residual value carried by a named assignee. Lawful exit: each harbor’s required final filings and deregistrations are completed. For an institution, this retires the last harbor, a transition §12’s corridor grammar cannot express because exit-prepare presumes a receiving jurisdiction. Record custody: a named custodian retains \sigma for a specified term and keeps it verifiable after runtime termination through Definition 4.4’s re-anchoring construction. The terminal receipt: a signed terminal digest over the chain head closes \sigma. Any later append is invalid by construction and detectable as forgery. A chain without this receipt has no recorded end. The receipt records sovereign dissolution or deregistration acts but does not constitute them (§17.1).

Definition 4.2 (Jurisdiction). A jurisdiction J = (E_J, L_J, K_J) consists of an evaluator function E_J that maps an asset state and a proposed action to a cell value on a named domain, a content-addressed lawpack L_J of type \mathrm{Lawpack} that E_J consults, and a signing key K_J under an epoch-rotation schedule. Because E_J takes the proposed action as an argument, a passing cell is in general a verdict about a class of actions rather than a standing fact about the asset. When E_J returns a passing cell on an action’s required domains, J emits an attestation recording the cell together with the scope of action parameters over which the evaluation holds (Definition 4.4). An attestation that omitted that scope would serialize an action-specific verdict as a standing one.

Definition 4.3 (Compliance domain). \mathcal{D} is a finite, evaluation-time-fixed set of regulatory domains. Every proposed action is indexed by a required subset D_\mathrm{req} \subseteq \mathcal{D}. The reference deployment instantiates \mathcal{D} with exactly twenty-three domains: anti-money-laundering, know-your-customer, sanctions, tax, securities, corporate governance, custody, data privacy, licensing, banking, payments, clearing, settlement, digital assets, employment, immigration, intellectual property, consumer protection, arbitration, trade, insurance, anti-bribery, and Sharia compliance. Asset methodologies may define subdomains, oracle fields, and methodology-specific labels inside those domains; adding a new top-level domain is a coordinated schema-evolution event across participating jurisdictions.

Definition 4.4 (Attestation). An attestation \alpha = (J, d, v, P_\alpha, t_\mathrm{issue}, t_\mathrm{expire}, \epsilon, A_\mathrm{bind}, s_J) records that jurisdiction J evaluated domain d and assigned cell value v for asset A_\mathrm{bind} over the action scope P_\alpha, valid from t_\mathrm{issue} to t_\mathrm{expire} under cryptographic epoch \epsilon, signed by K_J with signature s_J over every preceding field.

The scope predicate P_\alpha is a decidable predicate on an action’s declared parameters, counterparty class, amount band, instrument type, and corridor, recording the conditions under which E_J reached the verdict v. Setting P_\alpha = \top, the identically-true predicate, marks a genuinely standing determination, one that holds for every action on A_\mathrm{bind} in domain d within the validity window; any narrower scope binds the verdict to the actions it was reached for. The asset-binding field A_\mathrm{bind} is the digest h_g of the intended asset’s genesis document: an attestation issued for one asset is not valid for another. Because amendments append to \sigma without changing h_g, the binding identifies the asset, not the asset’s current program state. In the companion Sovereign Jurisdiction Network paper’s terms, an attestation is a W3C Verifiable Credential under the eIDAS profile with these fields. The signing key is a composite under a specified combiner: an Ed25519 signature and an ML-DSA-65 post-quantum signature, verifying iff both component signatures verify.

Epoch policy and scheme retirement. The epoch \epsilon indexes a per-jurisdiction, append-only table of acceptable signature schemes and their policy periods. Scheme retirement adds a row. An attestation verifies under the policy in force when it was signed, independently of the policy at verification time. A jurisdiction with live attestations must have a nonempty table: a missing epoch makes an attestation unverifiable in a way indistinguishable from forgery.

Re-anchoring preserves an append-only archive through signature-scheme retirement. A broken scheme makes historical records signed under it unverifiable, while re-signing those records would violate append-only history. Instead, append a fresh witness over the old record’s digest, signed under the new scheme. The historical entry and original signature stay intact, and a verifier who no longer trusts the old scheme can follow the witness to one it trusts. Each generation anchors the previous one, allowing the archive to outlive any scheme without rewriting a byte. This cannot repair a signature forged before the witness was added. Bridging must therefore occur while the retiring scheme is still sound, as a scheduled operation rather than an emergency response.

Definition 4.5 (Corridor). A corridor C(J_1, J_2) = (R, \mu, \gamma, P_\mathrm{commit}) between jurisdictions specifies a destination-side re-evaluation set R \subseteq \mathcal{D}, a partial domain-recognition map \mu, grade-recognition maps \gamma, and a commitment protocol P_\mathrm{commit} that stages, finalizes, and compensates cross-zone operations: the companion Op paper’s signed commitment protocol operated under the companion Sovereign Jurisdiction Network paper’s finality-certificate obligations. Corridors are asymmetric in general; \mu and \gamma may be defined under a mutual-recognition agreement or under a regional body’s standing authorization.

Axiom 4.1 (Recognition floor). For every corridor, \gamma maps no cell that is non-passing under §6 to a passing one: a NonCompliant or Pending cell on the sending side is NonCompliant or Pending on the receiving side. Sanctions lies in R of every corridor formed without an explicit shared-authority instrument over sanctions lists; this is the general-purpose corridor rule under which §3.1 re-evaluates Sanctions locally, and where such an instrument exists the first clause still binds.

The claim program also determines who may hold the asset, which carried evidence a receiving jurisdiction may rely on, and what the asset is worth. These quantities complete the object specification. They are read from \mathcal{V}(\Phi) and the compliance function of §6. Fix an asset A at time t, issued by an institution with harbor set H. Let \mathcal{N} be the candidate counterparties, each an institution with its own harbor set and attestation portfolio.

Definition 4.6 (Admissible holder set). The admissible holder set \mathrm{Hold}_t(A) is the set of e \in \mathcal{N} such that the transfer action a_e moving A to e has a passing verdict at t: every domain in D_\mathrm{req}(a_e) is passing in A’s action-scoped tensor T_{a_e}, every domain in D_\mathrm{req}(a_e) is passing in e’s, and the corridor joining e’s harbors to H admits the transfer under Definition 4.5 without obstruction. It is the image of \Phi’s transfer transition under that filter: computed, not declared, and moving whenever either side’s tensor moves. A sanctions transition in one harbor removes counterparties from \mathrm{Hold}_t(A) before any manual review, which is the formal content of the flag narrated in §3.8: a cargo that has crossed a designated party’s custody clears no further downstream transfer until re-evaluated.

Definition 4.7 (Reliance class). For a receiving jurisdiction J_2 and a corridor C(J_1, J_2) = (R, \mu, \gamma, P_\mathrm{commit}), the reliance class \mathrm{Rel}_t(A, J_2) is the image under \gamma of the attestations A carries at t on the domains \mu maps, together with the domains of R marked as requiring fresh evaluation on the receiving side. It records what a party in J_2 may rely on without re-executing the evaluation, and at what grade, and what it may not. Domains outside both \mu and R carry no reliance: by the fail-closed discipline of §6 they stand at Pending in J_2.

Definition 4.8 (Clearing price). Let Q be a pricing measure on the observables \Phi names and let D be a discount process, both supplied by the model under which the holder population prices. The clearing price \mathrm{Price}_t(A) is the value process \mathcal{V}(\Phi) at t under (Q, D): the Q-expectation of the discounted future cash flows, conditional on the information available at t, taken on the branches \Phi’s gate and hole constructors leave live at t. The holder set enters the price twice: a branch whose continuation requires a transition the §6 verdict refuses, a transfer to a counterparty outside \mathrm{Hold}_t(A) among them, is a branch some gate constructor zeroes; and Q prices over the population \mathrm{Hold}_t(A), the only counterparties who can lawfully take the other side of a trade. The channel by which a shrinking holder set moves the price through Q is the liquidity question the companion Pricing the Compliance Tensor takes up.

Definition 4.8 specifies valuation without asserting an existence theorem. Uniqueness of Q, completeness of the market over \mathrm{Hold}_t(A), and attainment of a price in trade remain questions about the holder population. The companion Pricing the Compliance Tensor studies the conditioning’s order structure. The construction fixes the direction of dependence: a tensor transition can shrink \mathrm{Hold}_t(A) and zero a branch of \mathcal{V}(\Phi), both computable from signed state. A price movement does neither. Compliance state therefore enters valuation as an input.

Matching, settlement, risk reservation, dispute, and recovery over these three objects belong to the clearing layer. Its construction remains open (§17.4).

The genesis record and claim-program grammar require one further definition: the authority gate admitting consequential transitions, including amendments.

Definition 4.9 (Authority gate). An authority gate is a triple \theta = (D_\theta, \pi_\theta, \tau_\mathrm{proc}): a domain scope D_\theta \subseteq \mathcal{D}, an identity predicate \pi_\theta on authorized signers, and a process type \tau_\mathrm{proc} in the sense of Definition 11.1. The gate admits a proposed transition a at time t iff the verdict of §6 on a over D_\theta is passing in the action-scoped tensor T_a, and \sigma carries a PCAuth for the gate, an authorization site in the sense of Definition 11.2, whose signer satisfies \pi_\theta and whose process-provenance record is consistent with \tau_\mathrm{proc}. A gate is stricter than the compliance gate of Definition 9.3, which independently requires the action’s own D_\mathrm{req}(a), in exactly two ways: it may require domains beyond the action’s own, and it always requires a signature. A passing verdict alone admits nothing through a gate.

The genesis bullet of Definition 4.1 can now be discharged. The amendment rule fixed by g is: a transition of amendment kind, one whose successor state resolves a new amendable record (\Phi', \Lambda', p') of claim program, program family, and authoring principal, is admitted only when \theta_\ast admits it under Definition 4.9 and every predicate in \mathcal{I} holds on (\Phi', \Lambda', p'). Both conjuncts are mechanical: \theta_\ast’s admission is a §6 verdict plus a signature check, and \mathcal{I} is a set of decidable predicates. The rule is keyed to h_g, neither \mathcal{I} nor \theta_\ast lies in the range of any amendment record, and \pi_{\theta_\ast} names principal keys, never the runtime’s receipt-signing key.

Proposition 4.1 (Mandate invariance). Let A have genesis g with invariant set \mathcal{I} and amendment gate \theta_\ast, and let the genesis record (\Phi_0, \Lambda_0, p_0) satisfy \mathcal{I}. Then every amendable record (\Phi_n, \Lambda_n, p_n) reachable from genesis by runtime-admitted transitions satisfies \mathcal{I}; the rule under which amendments are admitted is the same at every n; and no admitted chain of transitions installs an authoring principal without a signature satisfying \pi_{\theta_\ast}, which no program handle in any \Lambda_i can produce.

Proof. Induction on the length of the admitted chain. The base case is the hypothesis on (\Phi_0, \Lambda_0, p_0). For the step, assume (\Phi_i, \Lambda_i, p_i) satisfies \mathcal{I} and consider the next admitted transition. If it is not of amendment kind, it leaves the amendable record fixed, since only amendment records resolve a new (\Phi, \Lambda, p) (Definition 4.1). If it is of amendment kind, the amendment rule admits it only if (\Phi_{i+1}, \Lambda_{i+1}, p_{i+1}) satisfies \mathcal{I}, which is the inductive claim. The rule itself is invariant along the chain: it is keyed to h_g, amendments append to \sigma and do not edit g (Definition 4.1), and \mathcal{I} and \theta_\ast lie outside the range of every amendment record, so no admitted transition reaches a state governed by a different rule. For the last clause, an amendment installing p_{i+1} \neq p_i requires a PCAuth whose signature satisfies \pi_{\theta_\ast}. Program handles are content digests with type signatures (Definition 4.1); they hold no signing keys, and the runtime signs receipts under its own key, which \pi_{\theta_\ast} does not name. A chain of admitted transitions can extend \Lambda, but each extension passes the same amendment rule and equips \Lambda only with handles, never with a key satisfying \pi_{\theta_\ast}. So the witness for principal succession is produced outside the asset’s own program family at every link of the chain. \square

The proposition bounds admission independently of key custody. A compromised principal key (Assumption 5.2) can authorize any amendment the rule admits, but none that violates \mathcal{I}. It cannot replace \mathcal{I} or \theta_\ast, since no amendment reaches them. Compromise thus affects only what the invariant set leaves amendable. Quorum, timing, and veto discipline above \theta_\ast remain governance choices (§17.4), while the invariant holds for every such choice.

5. Adversary and Trust Model

Assumption 5.1 (Cryptographic). The adversary is computationally bounded and cannot forge signatures under the composite scheme, find hash collisions, or compromise current-epoch private keys of sovereign evaluators.

Assumption 5.2 (Adversary powers). The adversary may delay messages within bounded asynchrony \Delta (in the sense of a partial-synchrony model); compromise a bounded subset of oracle feeds; propose malformed actions; cause a single evaluator to equivocate, presenting conflicting attestations for the same jurisdiction, domain, and asset to different observers; attempt to replay expired or revoked attestations; inject adversarial content into a delegated proposal program’s context via controlled oracle channels; influence the authoring of a lawpack prior to its content-addressing; compromise an authoring principal’s key post-genesis; and equivocate as a hosted-execution provider against pinned runtime-identity attestations.

Definition 5.3 (Scoped trust). Accepting an attestation signed by J under lawpack version L for domain d and asset A means: trusting that J’s designated evaluator correctly decided d under L for A within the validity window. No statement about J’s correctness outside this scope is implied. Scoped trust is a definition of acceptance in this architecture, not an assumption about the world.

Under this model, the paper establishes seven guarantees: attestation unforgeability (5.1), historical receipt integrity (chain structure), NonCompliant non-overridability on required domains (Remark 6.1), and passing-preflight admission (§9 Proposition 9.1). It also establishes amendment-chain invariance (§4 Proposition 4.1), cross-asset PCAuth replay prevention (Definition 11.2 binding), and a fresh-signature requirement for re-authorizing suspended delegation (§10 monotonic-suspension). These guarantees do not establish evaluator correctness, since scoped trust defines acceptance rather than guaranteeing correct evaluation. Resistance to evaluator collusion, model-provider silent substitution without verifiable weights commitments, and lawpack corruption at authoring remain outside them (§17). So does elimination of race conditions in non-monotone channels beyond the specified invariants.

6. The Compliance Function

The compliance function is a typed object on the asset’s state: a static composition of cell values, and a dynamic propagation on a labeled directed acyclic graph of domains (§7). The static algebra is the composition algebra of the companion Multi-Harbored Institution paper, imported here in the form the runtime uses.

Each compliance domain carries a cell with two axes. Applicability takes Applicable, NotApplicable, or Exempt; compliance applies only when Applicable and takes NonCompliant < Pending < Compliant. On cells whose applicability agrees, composition is the meet: the strictest verdict binds, and NonCompliant absorbs. When applicability disagrees across jurisdictions, composition is an n-ary reduction, not a lattice meet: it returns the meet of the Applicable grades with the non-applicable inputs retained as provenance flags, or a named obstruction when no input is Applicable and the inputs mix NotApplicable with Exempt. Obstructions are absorbing under further reduction and are surfaced, never coerced.

Remark 6.1 (Non-overridability). A required domain that is NonCompliant in any harbor cannot be outvoted by other domains’ or other harbors’ evidence: NonCompliant absorbs under the meet, and by Axiom 4.1 no corridor carries it into a receiving harbor as passing. The floor is structural: a fact of the meet and of the recognition maps, holding in every harbor set.

The runtime applies a fail-closed write discipline to compliance state. At instantiation, each domain stands at (Applicable, Pending) unless a genesis attestation declares it out of scope. A cell write requires a valid attestation binding the asset, domain, value, action scope, validity window, cryptographic epoch, and signature (Definition 4.4). Expiry removes the attestation from standing state, returns cells justified only by it to Pending, and enqueues propagation (§7). A domain without a valid attestation therefore remains Pending and blocks any operation that requires it.

Fix a proposed action a. The action-scoped tensor T_a assigns to each pair (J, d) the meet of the cell values of those \alpha \in \pi issued by J on domain d for which P_\alpha(a) holds, and (Applicable, Pending) when no such attestation is held. Several attestations by one jurisdiction on one domain under different scopes therefore coexist in \pi without T_a ceasing to be a function of (J, d), and where two scopes both cover a the strictest applicable verdict binds, under the same meet that composes across harbors. Standing determinations (P_\alpha = \top) enter every action’s tensor; narrower ones enter only the actions whose parameters they were issued against, so a verdict reached for one counterparty class and one amount band is never silently reused for another.

Write T for the state each T_a is read off: the assignment sending a pair (J, d) to the scoped cells \pi currently holds there, every cell carried with the predicate P_\alpha it was issued under. Issuance, expiry, revocation, and the propagation of §7 move T; a preflight (Definition 8.1) obtains T_a from T by selecting the scopes the action satisfies and taking the meet. T is the asset’s standing evidence; only T_a is a verdict about an action.

A cell is passing for an operation if it is Applicable-and-Compliant, NotApplicable, or Exempt. The verdict on an action a over a required domain set composes the cells of T_a domain-by-domain and is passing iff every cell is passing and no obstruction occurred. For an asset owned by an institution with harbor set H = \{J_1, \ldots, J_n\}, the verdict is a two-step reduction: compose across harbors per domain, then verdict across domains.

On the in-scope fragment, the residual is Heyting implication. Because meet is restrictive, meeting a cell with current state cannot improve compliance. Remediation instead identifies the attestations, proof refreshes, or discretion-hole fills that must replace stale or failing cells through authorized state transitions.

7. The Propagation Graph

The dynamic half of the compliance function records the causal dependencies among compliance domains.

Definition 7.1 (Propagation graph). A propagation graph is a triple G = (\mathcal{D}, E, \lambda) with \mathcal{D} a finite domain set, E \subseteq \mathcal{D} \times \mathcal{D} a set of directed edges, and \lambda a labeling assigning to each edge the cell-transitions that trigger target-side re-evaluation. An edge (u, v) \in E_J records that J’s evaluator for v reads the value of u composed across harbors under §6, not J’s own cell at u alone, so a transition of u in any harbor moves an input of the cell (J, v), and E_J holds an edge for every source J’s evaluator for v reads: E_J is that evaluator’s read relation. Each per-harbor graph G_J is required to be acyclic. The institutional graph G_H is the labeled edge union across harbors after a validation pass that rejects harbor sets whose edge-union cycles; the composed-source reading is what lets the union carry a dependency from one harbor into another and makes a cycle in the union a cycle in fact. When a cycle is detected, the corridor-formation protocol requires negotiation or strongly-connected-component quotienting.

Proposition 7.1 (Propagation closure). Fix a finite DAG G_H and let domain d transition in at least one harbor. Let \mathrm{reach}(d) = \{d' : d \preceq d' \text{ in } G_H\}, and let the re-evaluation set be the set of cells (J, d') with (u, d') \in E_J for some u \in \mathrm{reach}(d): in each harbor, the targets of that harbor’s own edges whose source lies in \mathrm{reach}(d). Re-evaluating exactly this set, in rounds that follow a topological order of G_H, yields a tensor in which every cell’s justification reads the post-transition composed value of every source its rule names, and touches no cell outside \mathrm{reach}(d). Reach is computable in time O(|\mathcal{D}| + |E_H|) and the re-evaluation set in one further pass over the per-harbor edge lists. Re-evaluation terminates in at most |\mathcal{D}| closure rounds; each round’s operational cost is the evaluator-response latency for the touched cells.

Proof. Re-evaluate the cells of the re-evaluation set one topological level of G_H per round: every domain at the same longest-path distance from d, in all harbors, in one round. A source in \mathrm{reach}(d) of a domain at distance k lies at a distance below k, since any path from d to the source extends along the edge into the domain, so every cell is re-evaluated after every source its rule reads has settled. Let (J, x) be any cell and u any source its rule reads, so (u, x) \in E_J by the edge-label semantics of Definition 7.1. If the composed value of u is unchanged, the justification of (J, x) stands. If it changed, then either u = d or some cell (J', u) was re-evaluated in this pass, which happens only when (w, u) \in E_{J'} for some w \in \mathrm{reach}(d); in both cases u \in \mathrm{reach}(d), since E_{J'} \subseteq E_H. Then (u, x) \in E_J with u \in \mathrm{reach}(d) places (J, x) in the re-evaluation set, and it was re-evaluated after u settled. The harbor J is selected by its own edge into x, whether or not it carries any edge at d: with G_{J_1} = \{d \to e\} and G_{J_2} = \{e \to f\}, the cell (J_2, f) reads the composed value of e that (J_1, e) moves, and it is re-evaluated because (e, f) \in E_{J_2} with e \in \mathrm{reach}(d). Conversely a cell (J, x) with x \notin \mathrm{reach}(d) has no source in \mathrm{reach}(d), since an edge (u, x) \in E_J \subseteq E_H with u \in \mathrm{reach}(d) would place x in \mathrm{reach}(d); it is untouched. Reachability on G_H is linear, and the re-evaluation set is one pass over the per-harbor edge lists testing membership of each source in \mathrm{reach}(d). A pass by level has exactly as many rounds as the longest directed path from d has edges, which in a DAG on \mathcal{D} is at most |\mathcal{D}| - 1, so at most |\mathcal{D}| rounds. \square

The complexity bound concerns the domain graph. Evaluator-response times dominate operational cost: reaching 15 domains across 4 harbors requires at most 60 calls, each with legal-process latency from minutes to days. Liveness under bounded evaluator latency requires timeouts. After a configurable timeout, unanswered cells return to Pending and the asset remains frozen or obstructed until the evaluator responds.

Concurrent transitions are serialized per-asset by the receipt-chain append lock; cross-asset transitions have their own ordering through P_\mathrm{commit} of Definition 4.5, the companion Op paper’s signed commitment protocol operated under the companion Sovereign Jurisdiction Network paper’s finality-certificate obligations.

8. Preflight and Postflight

The compliance gate operates at two moments bracketing every execution.

Definition 8.1 (Preflight snapshot). At the preflight instant for action a, the runtime produces a preflight snapshot: a content-addressed record capturing T_a over D_\mathrm{req}(a), the chain head, the relevant delegation state, and a monotonic logical clock value. Admission is atomic in the snapshot: either the snapshot is consumed to admit a, or a is rejected. Because T_a is assembled from the attestations whose scope predicate a satisfies, the snapshot records the scope under which admission was granted, not only the domains it cleared.

Discipline 8.1 (Monotone observation). Within a single execution, the runtime observes the tensor at the preflight snapshot and does not re-consult the live tensor during execution. Changes between preflight and postflight update the live tensor without retroactively altering the snapshot.

Discipline 8.2 (Postflight attribution). Every transition of the standing state T carries its provenance: the attestation, expiry, revocation, or receipt event that caused it (§6, §7). At the postflight instant the runtime re-reads the verdict on the live tensor. A non-passing domain is attributed to a when the transition that moved it lies in the propagation reach (Proposition 7.1) of an effect recorded in a’s own receipt, and is external otherwise. The response is fixed: an external failure leaves a’s effects standing and freezes the asset; a failure attributed to a appends a compensating receipt reverting a’s effects. Both responses append; neither rewrites history.

Proposition 8.1 (Preflight-postflight safety). If the preflight verdict is passing and a’s effects are deterministic in the snapshot, then a executes exactly once; the attribution of any postflight failure is decidable from \sigma, the attestation log, and G; and \sigma records which of Discipline 8.2’s two responses ran.

Proof. Admission consumes the preflight snapshot atomically (Definition 8.1), so a is admitted at most once, and a passing admitted a with snapshot-deterministic effects runs to a unique successor state; the receipt append is a compare-and-swap on the chain head (Definition 4.1) that commits exactly one receipt or transitions the asset to a fault state with no partial append. Attribution is a reachability question on G from the transitions a’s receipt records, decidable in O(|\mathcal{D}| + |E_H|) by Proposition 7.1 over the provenance the write discipline of §6 retains. The response receipts are appends under the same chain discipline, so the case that ran is legible from \sigma. \square

A frozen asset permits only re-evaluation programs: Level-1 gates that clear obstructions without Level-2 state changes. This lets it receive fresh attestations while other execution remains blocked.

9. The Smart Asset Virtual Machine

The Smart Asset Virtual Machine (SAVM) specializes the companion Op paper’s evaluator to asset-resident programs. Level-1 compliance gates are Op bytecode compiled from Lex rules under that paper’s admissible-compilation theorem. Level-2 programs are Op programs authored by principals or generated by delegated tooling under amendment governance. The machine is deterministic under the conditions in Definition 9.2.

Definition 9.1 (SAVM state). A SAVM state is \Sigma = (A, T, H, B, G), where A is the asset record, T is the standing evidence state of §6 over the current \pi projected through the current harbor set, from which every preflight reads the action-scoped tensor T_a the gate decides on, H is the harbor set resolved from the institutional pointer in g, B is the budget triple (gas, external calls, wall-clock) and G is the propagation graph for H. The asset record carries \Phi, and the machine consults it: the set of \lambda_i \in \Lambda the machine may attempt at a given lifecycle state is the set whose transition kind lies on a branch that \Phi’s gate and hole constructors leave live at that state, read under T and \sigma. Definition 9.3 then decides the attempt.

Definition 9.2 (SAVM transition). An invocation of program p with input x at state \Sigma computes

\mathrm{Step}_p : (\Sigma, x) \mapsto (\Sigma', r, y)

where \Sigma' is the successor state, r is the execution receipt, and y is the output. \mathrm{Step}_p is deterministic conditional on (i) the oracle-attestation vector supplied as preconditions to the call and (ii) the wall-clock deadline enforcement that may halt execution when the deadline is exceeded. The paper’s claim is therefore “deterministic in the oracle-attestation vector up to deadline-termination”; replay of a receipt requires preserving both the oracle vector and the termination boundary.

Definition 9.3 (Compliance-gated invocation). Let a be the action an invocation of p on input x proposes. Gated invocation admits the step only when, for every d \in D_\mathrm{req}(p), the composed cell of the preflight action-scoped tensor T_a at d is passing; otherwise it emits a reject receipt with no state change. An attestation whose scope predicate a fails contributes nothing to that verdict, so a determination reached for a different counterparty or a different amount band cannot admit a.

Proposition 9.1 (Gate witness). If gated invocation produces a non-\bot output, the preflight verdict was passing and the execution is recorded in \sigma with a chain-integrity receipt.

Proof. By Definitions 9.3 and 4.1: the only path to a non-\bot output runs through the passing branch; the receipt-chain append is a compare-and-swap against the chain head that either commits or triggers the runtime’s fault handling. \square

The witness establishes passing admission and a recorded execution. It is not an end-to-end safety theorem and does not establish verdict correctness, delegated-output scope containment, or freshness of T at preflight. Section 10 specifies scope containment, and §11 supplies the uniqueness discipline for principal-carried authorization (Axiom 11.1, Proposition 11.1). Section 17 distinguishes verdict validity from correctness.

The budget triple bounds deterministic computation through gas, external-service invocation through call limits, and evaluator-response and real-time operations through wall-clock limits. Execution terminates when the first budget is exhausted.

9.1 Level structure

Level-1 programs are compiled compliance-enforcement primitives bound to the asset by content digest. Level-2 action logic covers rebalancing, harbor-transition proposals, reporting, negotiation, and delegated proposal execution. It runs only after Level-1 verdicts pass. A Level-2 attempt to alter compliance state fails structurally: tensor updates require sovereign-signed attestations that Level-2 programs cannot forge.

9.2 Negotiation (via Op’s signed commitment protocol)

Two intelligent assets negotiating an exchange run the signed commitment protocol specified in the companion Op and Sovereign Jurisdiction Network papers: one phase locks terms and resources, one phase collects verdicts, and finality evidence determines commit or compensation. Op’s Locked<T, ω, ε> typestate carries each asset’s lockable interest through the commit window; failure on either side triggers compensation or a typed obstruction. The full cross-zone atomicity statement is an open protocol obligation under the finality assumptions stated in those papers.

10. Delegated Proposal Programs as Bound Programs

Delegated proposal programs are bound programs under the Level-2 class. Their integration requires specific additions to the handle type, the authorization model, the execution broker, and the receipt discipline.

10.1 Proposal-program handle

Definition 10.1 (Delegated proposal-program handle). A delegated proposal-program handle content-addresses a quintuple (r, q, \mathcal{T}, \delta, \beta) with:

  • r a runtime binding: a signed statement pinning the execution provider, runtime family, version, policy hash, and the provider’s current-attested integrity commitment where such a commitment is exposed. The architecture uses a provider attestation when bit-exact runtime commitments are unavailable; when verifiable commitments become available, the binding is strengthened without changing the surface. An equivalence-class predicate is permitted, trading bit-exact reproducibility for operational tractability against provider deprecation cycles.
  • q a frozen instruction and scaffold, content-addressed.
  • \mathcal{T} a typed tool scope: the finite set of operations the program may invoke, each with parameter types and required-domain sets.
  • \delta the principal’s signed delegation to this program instance.
  • \beta the budget triple (gas, external calls, wall-clock) governing this program’s invocations.

A change to r, q, \mathcal{T}, \delta, or \beta is a new handle and requires a signed amendment. Equivalence-class predicates reduce the frequency of principal-signature ceremony under provider deprecation; under an equivalence-class binding, provider deprecation within the class is a valid continuation; deprecation outside the class requires a signed amendment.

Model identity in the verdict path. The identity discipline for execution also applies to evidence used in evaluation. When a model helps produce a verdict, commit must pin its identity, a content digest of its weights, and its decode parameters, just as evaluation pins the lawpack version. A verdict states what the rules required at a particular time. Silently changing the rule set underlying that statement makes it unfalsifiable. Re-running an old case under a newer model produces a new observation, advisory unless an authority adopts it. It does not correct the historical verdict, which records the state of the art at its own date. Re-evaluation under a superseding lawpack has the same structure: a new derived consequence leaves the frozen historical fact intact. The verdict function itself must remain deterministic. Model contributions are evidence inputs on which a human or rule acts, outside the function that computes the verdict. A stochastic component there would prevent the replay the receipt chain supports.

10.2 Delegation and revocation

The delegation \delta uses macaroon-style capabilities (biscuits) with caveats. It names the program’s public-key identity, the permitted operations, the permitted resources, per-action and per-window monetary limits, the admissible discretion holes, the validity window, and the revocation state. The revocation state is monotone in suspension: an active delegation may transition to cooling, suspended, or revoked; it may not transition back. Returning to active requires a fresh signed delegation. Revocation propagates to admission-time checks within the bounded asynchrony of Assumption 5.2; any in-flight program state terminates at the broker on revocation and is not resumed.

Each program action enters an append-only forensic index keyed by capability ID, with idempotent inserts. The index denormalizes canonical receipts for regulatory queries and helps discover actions. Enforcement remains with the tool scope, delegation caveats, and compliance gate.

10.3 Authorization graded by rollback cost

Role, scope, and monetary size are three axes of the authorization model. The cost of undoing an action is a fourth, orthogonal to the other three. Every operation in a tool scope \mathcal{T} carries a rollback class: reversible when a kernel action undoes its consequences and no effect crosses the asset’s boundary; hard to reverse when a compensating action recovers the position at a cost; irreversible when no kernel action undoes it, as with an external payment, a dismissal, a signed merger, a published filing. An operation whose class is undeclared is treated as irreversible.

Rollback class imposes different admission conditions on principals and programs. A human principal executes an irreversible action on role and scope alone. A machine-initiated irreversible action additionally requires a per-action human counter-signature bound to the receipt or an explicitly granted unilateral capability. That capability is intended to have a short validity window for operations where waiting for a counter-signature costs more than the risk it removes. These requirements are additive: sanctions, licensure, role, monetary, and scope checks still run independently and must pass.

The specified gate checks that the counter-signature artifact is present. Verification against the principal’s registered key remains open, together with per-action cryptographic binding, a cooling period after delegation issuance, and dual control for the most sensitive irreversible classes. These are engineering obligations with the same status as the provider-attestation binding of §10.1.

10.4 Execution broker, context assembly, and the proposer-gate relationship

The execution broker handles all program-kernel interactions. The program reads through typed tools (including a read-only tensor view, a residual-computation tool, a corridor-listing tool, an attestation-request tool, and an escalation tool for discretion holes) and writes through typed tools whose execution is subject to admission. Every tool invocation is scope-checked against \delta before admission. The program has no direct database access; the broker holds delegations and presents only the public summary to the program.

The program is the proposer, and the compliance gate is the admission filter. An admitted proposal makes the program the action’s proximate programmatic cause. A refusal is recorded with the proposal. The distinction concerns evidence: the gate tests program outputs against a tensor computed from sovereign-signed attestations, and never uses those outputs to set compliance state. Proposal outputs do not update the kernel’s world model. The receipt retains their rationale only as an input artifact under the selected disclosure profile. The gate blocks compliance-failing proposals. Compliance-passing proposals may still be substantively unwise, and liability remains with the authoring principal whose delegation authorized the program.

10.5 Adversarial input and multi-step workflow defenses

Adversarial input is a realistic channel (Assumption 5.2). The architectural mitigations are:

  • Input partitioning: attacker-controlled content (counterparty inputs, oracle payloads derived from user content) is rendered in the program context with a structural marker that both the program and the broker can enforce; tool calls that reference content from attacker-controlled partitions carry a taint bit that propagates.
  • Dual-parser separation for high-stakes flows: a quarantine parser extracts structured facts from attacker-controlled content; a privileged program reasons only over the extracted structured facts.
  • Scope-narrowing against injected directives: the tool scope \mathcal{T} and the per-action caps in \delta bound the damage of a successful injection to the intersection of (compliance-passing actions) \cap (delegated actions); the size of this intersection is a deployment design parameter.

Multi-step workflows (search -> summarize -> draft -> review -> settle) admit a choice between per-step gates (safer, higher latency) and settlement-only gates (lower latency, admits mid-chain information leakage). The architecture admits both; the paper does not prescribe a policy. The delegation \delta may require per-step gating for high-stakes tool classes and settlement-only gating for low-stakes classes.

10.6 The rationale record on the receipt chain

The rationale record includes more than a proposal’s potentially confidential explanation. Along with the model identity and decode parameters of §10.1, it binds the stated plan’s digest and maps each plan step to the executed program range it produced. A stochastic invocation cannot be replayed, so audit compares the frozen plan with the frozen executed program. The receipt supports this per-step comparison but does not verify conformance, which remains the proposal-program specification-conformance problem of §17.4. Three disclosure profiles are available: plaintext for public verification, regulator-only encryption at rest with supervisor-key disclosure (§13), and commit-and-reveal through a zero-knowledge accumulator for selective disclosure. Regulator-only is the default.

11. Typed Discretion Holes and Principal-Carried Authorization

A compliance rule is rarely algorithmic in its entirety. A typed discretion hole is the formal object carrying the escalation to a human or a collective principal.

Definition 11.1 (Discretion hole). A typed discretion hole \eta = (\tau_q, \tau_a, \pi_\mathrm{req}, \tau_\mathrm{proc}) is a query type, an answer type, an identity-predicate on authorized signers, and a process type specifying the governance procedure, single-signer, quorum-signed, deliberation-windowed, ongoing-withdrawable, or a specified conjunction thereof. The process type is essential: the architecture distinguishes discretion holes filled by a licensed officer’s single-signature (e.g., a tax-election signature) from those filled under a structured consent process (e.g., Institutional Review Board approval, indigenous-council free-prior-informed consent, or a board vote with named quorum). A single cryptographic signature at a moment captures a stamp; the process type captures the deliberative protocol that makes the stamp legitimate under the relevant governance regime.

Definition 11.2 (Principal-carried authorization). A PCAuth is a tuple (k, \eta, a, t, A_\mathrm{bind}, \Pi, s_k) where k is the signing principal’s identifier (which may itself be a threshold-signature expression over multiple keys under the process type), \eta the authorization site, a discretion hole of Definition 11.1 or an authority gate of Definition 4.9 (a gate \theta authorizes as a site whose query is the proposed transition, whose answer type is approval, and whose identity predicate and process type are \pi_\theta and \tau_\mathrm{proc}), a : \tau_a(\eta) the answer, t the validity timestamp, A_\mathrm{bind} the asset digest, \Pi the process-provenance record (deliberation-window boundaries, consultation records, witness signatures where the process type requires them), and s_k the signature over (\eta, a, t, A_\mathrm{bind}, \Pi). Validity at time t_\mathrm{now} requires: the site’s identity predicate holds of k, s_k verifies, t_\mathrm{now} lies within the validity window, and \Pi is consistent with \eta’s process type.

Axiom 11.1 (At-most-once resumption). A discretion-hole encounter suspends the invocation that reached it. The runtime resumes a suspended invocation at most once; resumption consumes the encounter, and a second resumption of the same encounter is refused at admission.

Proposition 11.1 (PCAuth uniqueness per encounter). A discretion-hole encounter admits at most one PCAuth, and the resumption it authorizes is a distinct receipt in \sigma.

Proof. A PCAuth is checked and consumed only at resumption, where Definition 11.2’s validity conditions are evaluated. By Axiom 11.1 the encounter resumes at most once, so at most one PCAuth is consumed for it, and the resumption receipt records which. \square

The A_\mathrm{bind} field forecloses cross-asset PCAuth replay: a signed hole-fill for asset A is not a valid fill for asset B. The process-provenance record \Pi makes the procedural requirements of complex consent frameworks first-class: FPIC under Convention 169 of the International Labour Organization and the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples, Institutional Review Board review under national research-ethics regulations, quorum-voting records for board actions, and Nagoya-Protocol benefit-sharing arrangements all have a place on the receipt chain and a formal check on the SAVM’s admission path.

12. Corridors, Composition, and Harbor Transitions

When an asset lawfully adds a harbor J_2 or retires a harbor J_1, the corridor specifies which evidence travels. Domains in R require fresh evaluation. Domains mapped by \mu and accepted by \gamma carry under mutual recognition, while domains outside both default to Pending. The transition follows P_\mathrm{commit} of Definition 4.5: the companion Op paper’s signed commitment protocol under the Sovereign Jurisdiction Network paper’s finality-certificate obligations. A sending jurisdiction emits exit-prepare when an existing harbor retires. The receiving jurisdiction emits entry-prepare, evaluates R, and produces fresh attestations. Finality evidence places a corridor-finality receipt in both chains. On failure, the signed terminal state determines compensation, obstruction, or abort.

Across mixed-axis cells, composition is partial: obstructions halt the reduction and are reported. Route coherence of corridors is an explicit check over the re-evaluation masks, domain-recognition maps, grade-recognition maps, pack-version windows, and legal-instrument clauses. The working examples of §3 assume only those corridor behaviors for which the relevant route-coherence checks pass.

The corridor protocol interoperates with existing multilateral mechanisms at the level of attestation recognition:

  • Treaty composition: Multilateral Instrument treaty look-ups are evaluator inputs for tax domains; an attestation’s image under the grade-recognition map \gamma on a mapped tax cell carries through only when the relevant bilateral treaty recognizes the corresponding tax fact.
  • Information exchange: Common Reporting Standard and Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act reports are attestation-projection operations on the receipt chain’s financial-account view; an intelligent-asset treasury operating under these regimes produces compliant reports as a receipt-chain projection rather than as a separate pipeline.
  • BEPS Pillar-2: Global Anti-Base-Erosion top-up tax is handled by a tax-fiber workflow rather than ordinary compliance meet. The country-by-country-reporting cell, the Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax cell, and the Income Inclusion Rule cell supply signed inputs; GloBE interactions, safe-harbor elections, treaty positions, and top-up-tax allocation remain a formal tax-composition obligation. The Transitional Country-by-Country Reporting Safe Harbor election is a discretion-hole fill by the group tax head.
  • Paris Agreement Article 6: corresponding-adjustment attestations for internationally-transferred mitigation outcomes are attestations signed by the host jurisdiction’s designated national authority; they are first-class objects in the receipt chain, not derived.
  • EU eIDAS: attestations follow the W3C Verifiable Credential profile specified in the companion Sovereign Jurisdiction Network paper; eIDAS cross-border recognition under Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 (eIDAS 2.0) flows to these credentials.

Reducing harbor-transition compliance cost to proof verification and corridor-required re-evaluation lets entities respond faster to regulatory-quality signals. Cheaper exit intensifies existing competition, whether for quality or laxity. Under Remark 6.1 and Axiom 4.1, a failing Sanctions cell remains binding across harbors and corridors. The direction of competition depends on politics (§17).

13. The Supervisor’s Control Surface

Sovereigns exercise operational authority through supervisory primitives that take precedence over every principal delegation.

Definition 13.1 (Supervisory order). A supervisory order \omega = (K_\mathrm{sup}, \kappa, \tau, \Psi, s_\mathrm{sup}) is an order of kind \kappa (among: halt, unhalt, freeze-class, impose-requirement, reclassify-asset, mandate-disclosure, require-attestation-from) with parameters \Psi, addressed to a set of assets \tau in harbors under the supervisor’s authority K_\mathrm{sup}, signed by s_\mathrm{sup}.

Axiom 13.1 (Supervisor seniority). Supervisory orders are monotone over principal delegations: an authoring-principal delegation cannot override a supervisory halt. A supervisory unhalt can lift a supervisory halt; no principal authorization can.

Definition 13.2 (Late-binding requirement). When a supervisor requires compliance with a requirement not present at an asset’s genesis (an emergency regulation, a new sanctions list, a newly enforced right), the late-binding-requirement primitive: (1) attaches the requirement to one or more of the fixed twenty-three compliance domains, (2) injects initial tensor cells at (Applicable, Pending) for every affected asset inside those domains, (3) triggers propagation from the injected cells to downstream requirements, and (4) enforces the fail-closed default until sovereign evaluators issue attestations. Adding a new top-level domain to \mathcal{D} is a network schema-evolution event, not a per-asset supervisor order.

Definition 13.3 (Liability map). Every bound program’s execution receipt carries a liability tag identifying the responsible parties for each failure mode: the authoring principal (for program-authorship decisions), the evaluator (for attestation correctness within scoped trust), the oracle signer (for feed content), the hosted-execution provider (for proposal outputs within provider-attested-binding scope), and the corridor operator (for cross-zone commitment integrity). The failure modes, with their detection, loss bounds, and remedies, are enumerated in §17.5.

The supervisor can read the harbor’s full asset set. Each read carries its signature and enters an audit log separate from the asset’s receipt chain, so holders can verify access to their asset. Plaintext proposal rationales require a specific disclosure order.

14. Participant-as-Principal and Structured Consent

Scientific corpora, platform cooperatives, patient registries, and common-pool stewardships depend on many individual participants’ consent. The authoring-principal model alone cannot represent that consent. A participant-as-principal extension supplies it.

Definition 14.1 (Participant-principal tree). The authoring principal of a participant-governed asset is a Merkle-rooted participant-principal tree \mathcal{P}: each leaf is a participant’s public-key identifier and their individual consent record; the root is a commitment. Operations requiring participant authorization are composed under an operation-specific threshold function: some operations require a simple majority of affected leaves, some require every affected leaf’s signature, some delegate to an elected committee under the participant assembly’s own procedure.

Definition 14.2 (Consent as a structured cell). Consent is a first-class typed cell with fields: tiers (broad vs specific-to-study), purposes (research, commercial, statistical-training, internal-QA), geographic scopes (Applicable, NotApplicable, Exempt per destination harbor), temporal decay (validity window), withdrawal right (revocable, partially revocable, irrevocable on aggregation), and process-provenance (how the consent was obtained, in what language, with what information). A single cell like “data-privacy: Compliant” does not express this; the architecture widens the cell schema for object classes where consent structure is load-bearing.

Withdrawal propagation. A participant or authorized delegate signs withdrawal as an oracle attestation. It propagates over the regulatory-domain graph to flag cells for re-evaluation and over the derivative-use graph to mark dependent datasets, trained-model-weights, published aggregates, and downstream licensees. The derivative-use graph may contain cycles, so fixed-point termination is not guaranteed. Propagation is best-effort within a named scope: active uses immediately, trained models at the next licensing cycle, and aggregates on public release. Where aggregation is irrevocable, that limit is explicit. Once data enters a statistical aggregate above an unlinkable threshold, withdrawal removes direct identifiability but leaves the participant’s marginal influence.

Institutional Review Board review is a process-typed discretion hole. The board’s approval is a PCAuth under a quorum-signed, deliberation-windowed process type. Protocol amendments are appended as additional PCAuths; the receipt chain carries the sequence.

Free, prior, and informed consent is a process-typed discretion hole whose deliberation window follows the community’s own governance. The community determines the deliberation’s form. The architecture records its outcome, witness signatures, and withdrawal rights as state. The community’s constitution also governs signing-key custody, including non-digital fallback paths. Every credit-issuance event evaluates the pre-committed benefit-sharing formula through composition on the receipt chain.

Nagoya-Protocol benefit-sharing for genetic resources originating in biodiversity-rich jurisdictions is a distinct evaluator surface with its own methodology fields and its own attestation composition onto the participating jurisdictions’ canonical-domain tensors.

15. An Object Taxonomy

Twelve object classes distinguish what an asset observes, which domains its actions require, how it settles and fails, and which programs it carries. Classes I, XI, and XII receive full descriptions because they govern the payments, receivables, and cargo lifecycles of §3.

  • Class I. Cross-border financial operator. Payments, remittance, working-capital provisioning across jurisdictions. It observes identity-verification feeds, sanctions lists, and transaction-monitoring feeds; its actions require AML, Sanctions, ConsumerProtection, Payments, Banking, and Tax, with foreign-exchange reporting a methodology field inside the Payments/Banking/Tax domains; it settles continuously; it carries compiled AML/sanctions gates plus a delegated proposal program for novel onboarding.
  • Class II. Collective investment vehicle. Fund, structured product, revenue-linked note. Observes portfolio marks, revenue attestations, and index feeds; requires securities, tax, custody, and sanctions; settles continuously with distribution events.
  • Class III. Contingent-rights instrument. Options on regulatory conditions, exercise on attested outcomes; requires IP, licensing, and trade.
  • Class IV. Islamic-finance instrument (sukuk). Sharia-certified structured cash flows; the required domains must include Sharia.
  • Class V. IP index instrument. Weighted index of IP-backed cash flows; requires IP and Trade, with withholding-tax treatment recorded as Tax-domain methodology fields.
  • Class VI. Experiential claim. Right to a named service at a specified time and identity; requires consumer protection and data privacy; retail exposure flags for supervisory review.
  • Class VII. Multi-jurisdictional treasury / in-house bank. Cross-harbor working-capital operations under BEPS Pillar-2, CRS, FATCA, and treaty regimes. Adjacent to incumbent enterprise-resource-planning and treasury systems via typed watcher-attestations and journal-entry projections.
  • Class VIII. Physical infrastructure asset. Data center, port, transmission line, generation facility. Sensor-fusion oracle under Byzantine quorum; split between physical-jurisdiction harbor (land) and operating-entity harbor; export-control domain for workload-bearing facilities; grid-interconnect harbor for energy-market participation.
  • Class IX. Consent-governed scientific or cultural corpus. Genomic database, music catalog, research dataset. Participant-as-principal (§14); structured-consent cells; IRB-integrated discretion holes; withdrawal propagation on the derivative-use graph; Nagoya-Protocol benefit-sharing for applicable genetic resources.
  • Class X. Natural-system steward. Rainforest, fishery, aquifer, coral reef, mangrove belt. Multi-sovereign compact including recognized indigenous-council harbor under the council’s own governance; FPIC as process-typed discretion hole; buffer-pool-backed permanence across the methodology’s asset pool; leakage accounting through adjacent-tract monitoring; Article-6 corresponding-adjustment attestations for cross-border credit flows.
  • Class XI. Trade-finance receivable and receivables pool. Origination-time-wrapped bilateral trade instruments and their aggregated pools. The class observes shipment and bill-of-lading attestations, customs attestations, sanctions and export-control feeds, counterparty identity-verification attestations, and bank-signed letter-of-credit chains. Its actions require AML, Sanctions, Trade, Payments, Banking, Tax, and KYC; export-control, foreign-exchange reporting, customs, and counterparty-identity are methodology fields inside those domains. Settlement is finality-on-shipment-confirmation at origination and tranched-with-continuous-distribution at the pool layer. The class carries compiled Level-1 gates for the seven required domains plus Level-2 pool-management programs for concentration-limit enforcement, expected-loss computation under the methodology, and investor-tranche settlement via Op signed commitment. Pool tranching, senior / junior / first-loss waterfalls, over-collateralization, concentration limits per obligor and per corridor, dilution protection for investor entries and exits, capacity caps, is expressed through Level-2 programs composed over the pool’s receipt chain; credit-event semantics are explicit: a receivable in default transitions to a NonCompliant compliance state under the pool methodology’s domain, subordination is enforced at distribution-computation time, and recovery flows append to the pool’s receipt chain as attested proceeds. The class is structurally distinct from Class II (collective investment) in its origination-time attestation discipline and from Class III (contingent-rights instrument) in its settlement pattern; it is the specific instantiation on which §3.7 and §18.2 turn.
  • Class XII. Physical commodity in transit. A cargo, oil, metals, grain, LNG, petrochemicals, as an intelligent asset whose state carries origin, quality, custody-chain, shipping, insurance, and dispute-resolution documentation. The class observes independent-surveyor feeds (SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek), bill-of-lading chains and their electronic equivalents under eUCP version 2.1 or Bolero, customs attestations at load and discharge, flag-state registry, port authorities, marine-insurance-binder attestations from Lloyd’s syndicates, voyage-tracking from AIS, and sanctions and export-control feeds. Its actions require Sanctions, Trade, KYC, Custody, Insurance, and Arbitration; export-control, origin declaration, cargo quality, counterparty know-your-customer, and chain-of-custody integrity are methodology fields inside those domains. Settlement is structured against delivery milestones (load, in-transit, discharge, final-acceptance) with settlement conditioned on each milestone’s attestation; paired hedges (forward freight agreements, futures, options) compose under Op’s multi-asset signed commitment. On failure the class freezes on sanctions or chain-of-custody transition, compensates where the hedge’s reconciliation closes independently, and reverts on pre-discharge failure; it carries Level-1 gates for the six required domains plus Level-2 monitoring and dispute-resolution programs. The class operates under a chain-of-custody propagation graph orthogonal to the regulatory-domain graph of §7; transitions in either graph can flag the other for re-evaluation, and secondary-sanctions propagation runs through the custody graph rather than only through the domain graph. Arbitration-forum selection is a process-typed discretion hole under the admissible forum set (GAFTA, FOSFA, LMAA, LME, LCIA, ICC, SIAC, English High Court, New York Supreme Court). Class XII is structurally distinct from Class XI’s purely-financial receivables.

Classes can overlap. A hyperscale data center with consented telemetry for environmental claims is Class VIII and Class IX at different layers. A trade-receivables pool financing a Belt-and-Road infrastructure project combines Class VIII and Class XI. A cargo paired with a forward-freight derivative composes Class XII with Class III. The taxonomy supplies a vocabulary for these combinations rather than a disjoint partition.

16. Prior Art

The construction combines work on institutions, financial contracts, verified execution, and legal authority. Each tradition supplies a different part of the object.

Institutional economics. Douglass North’s Institutions, Institutional Change and Economic Performance (1990) establishes that institutions reduce transaction costs and that institutional change compounds over generations. Intelligent assets apply this thesis to instruments. Their algebra targets cross-jurisdictional transaction costs, while receipts make institutional change auditable at shorter intervals than North’s empirical record. Oliver Williamson’s transaction-cost economics studies cross-organization coordination friction. The architecture gives his hierarchies-vs-markets distinction a computational form. Elinor Ostrom’s Governing the Commons (1990) identifies eight design principles for common-pool-resource institutions. Seven have formal counterparts here: boundaries in harbor sets, congruent rules in composed tensors, monitoring in oracles and propagation graphs, graduated sanctions in lattice ordering and obstruction semantics, conflict resolution in discretion-hole escalation, recognition to organize in principal delegation, and nested enterprises in multi-harbor composition. The eighth, collective-choice arenas, remains institutional and external to the architecture.

Hirschman’s exit-voice framework. Exit, Voice, and Loyalty (1970) explains organizational decline and recovery through these three responses. Cheap cross-jurisdictional exit strengthens the exit channel. Amendment governance, discretion-hole fills, and participant-principal trees support voice. Loyalty is the rate of exit discounted by institutional attachment. The architecture lowers exit’s price while leaving that attachment unchanged. Tiebout’s model of local-jurisdiction choice (1956) supplies the corresponding price model: mobile constituents sort by rule quality, and cheaper exit sharpens that sorting.

Smart contracts and their verification. Lessig’s Code and Other Laws of Cyberspace and the verification literature (KEVM; F* translation of Solidity; Sergey and Hobor on concurrent interactions) study code-as-law and isolated-code safety. An intelligent asset additionally requires execution to pass a composed compliance verdict. Necula’s proof-carrying code is the closest precedent: an artifact carries proof of a property required by its consumer. The receipt chain descends from Haber and Stornetta’s hash-linked time-stamping. Move’s linear resources, Cairo’s ZK-provable execution, and Vyper’s verification work extend this tradition. Typed discretion holes and PCAuth are adjacent formalizations to Move’s resource types.

Financial-contract description languages. The term structure of \Phi comes from Peyton Jones, Eber, and Seward’s contract combinators (ICFP 2000), using Peyton Jones and Eber’s revised grammar (2003). Their compositional algebra contains zero, one, give, and, or, cond, scale, when, anytime, and until. Its denotation is a value process under a model for the observables. Section 4’s \mathrm{ClaimProgram} adds two constructors to that algebra, whose commercial descendants include LexiFi’s contract-description language and pricing systems. The lifecycle kinds ranged over by \lambda_i \in \Lambda are issuance, attestation, transfer, partial settlement, dispute, recovery, revocation, amendment, and termination. Where they overlap with the ISDA Common Domain Model, issuance maps to contract formation, transfer to transfer, amendment to terms change, and termination to termination. Attestation, revocation, dispute, and recovery carry the sovereign layer and have no Common Domain Model counterpart. FpML supplies the industrial product representation. This architecture adds two features absent from those predecessors. A lifecycle transition depends on jurisdiction-indexed attestation state: the term and observables still govern admissible transitions, with the action-scoped tensor of §6 as an additional condition. An external sovereign signature decides admissibility independently of the contract parties and the term’s own semantics, making the term portable across jurisdictions. The contribution at the claim layer is this gating of the inherited algebra.

Programmable securities. ERC-1400, ERC-1404, and ERC-3643 (T-REX) impose transfer restrictions through on-chain compliance modules. Zetzsche, Arner, and Buckley; Cong and He; and Harvey, Ramachandran, and Santoro’s DeFi work describe the product layer above these standards. Central Bank Digital Currency experiments (mBridge, Project Agora, Project Helvetia) prototype multi-jurisdictional programmable money. Tokenized real-world-asset platforms (Centrifuge, Ondo, RealT) implement particular asset classes. Intelligent assets define a primitive below these systems, composing sovereign attestations directly in place of per-token compliance modules.

Delegated automation. Capability-security systems, proof-carrying code, workflow engines, and typed effect systems supply the conceptual frame for scoped delegated programs. The architecture treats every such program as a proposer whose outputs enter the same admission filter as any other caller.

Environmental governance. The Integrity Council for the Voluntary Carbon Market’s Core Carbon Principles and the Voluntary Carbon Markets Integrity Initiative’s Claims Code of Practice set integrity frameworks for contemporary voluntary carbon markets. Article 6 of the Paris Agreement (6.2 ITMOs, 6.4 centralized mechanism) governs international mitigation-outcome transfers and corresponding adjustments to nationally-determined contributions. The UNFCCC REDD+ framework specifies institutions for project-scale and jurisdictional-scale forest carbon. The Nagoya Protocol on Access and Benefit-Sharing governs benefit flows for genetic resources. Class X operates within these frameworks and depends on their governance.

Legal theory. H. L. A. Hart’s Concept of Law distinguishes primary and secondary rules and identifies the open texture that requires judicial interpretation. Typed discretion holes formalize this boundary for execution. Dworkin’s Taking Rights Seriously and Law’s Empire, on principles versus rules and constructive interpretation, inform §17’s limits and lie outside the admissible fragment. Luhmann’s systems theory of law and Teubner’s reflexive law frame interaction with legal sub-systems. Kelsen’s pure theory of law supplies the rule-hierarchy precedent for lawpack versioning.

High-risk automated systems regulation. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 classifies automated systems deployed in financial decisioning and critical infrastructure as high-risk. Its obligations include human oversight (Article 14), transparency (Article 13), risk management, and incident reporting. Delegated programs operate within these obligations. Scope gates and forensic indexes support technical transparency and human oversight, while receipt-chain rationales and delegation revocation support incident reporting. Singapore’s Monetary Authority Veritas framework addresses the same requirements through fairness, ethics, accountability, and transparency.

Prior-period compliance frameworks. Financial Action Task Force Recommendation 16 (travel rule), the Basel Accords, and EU investment-services and banking passporting supply parts of the regulatory framework. e-Estonia X-Road and Cosmos Inter-Blockchain Communication supply adjacent technical constructions. Schrems II and the transatlantic Privacy Shield and Data Privacy Framework address related data-transfer questions. Each addresses part of the problem. The intelligent-asset construction composes across these regimes algebraically, beyond operation within any single one.

17. Limitations and Open Problems

Four kinds of limits govern the construction: admissibility preconditions, permanent features, political preconditions, and engineering tradeoffs. The object requires external bindings for legal force. Other limits concern what cryptography and formal methods can certify even after those bindings exist.

17.1 Admissibility Preconditions

Legal force. Legal regimes may reference this infrastructure. Without sovereign recognition of the attestation chain, signatures establish provenance but cannot alter legal status. This dependence is a legal binding condition rather than unfinished engineering. Deployment pilots must begin with no-action letters, regulatory-sandbox participation, or treaty instruments that reference the attestation format.

Mixed-axis structure remains open. The composition operation is algebraically complete on the in-scope fragment; across mixed-axis cells, the algebra returns named obstructions rather than lattice points. Whether the product admits a categorical generalization (a partial-Heyting structure with named obstruction classes, a distributive-lattice quotient under obstruction-absorption) is an open formal problem. The worked examples in §3 assume corridor behavior within the established region and annotate the assumption where it is load-bearing.

Bootstrapping coordination. Deployment requires a coalition of jurisdictions adopting compatible evaluator implementations. Its minimum size N is a deployment parameter this paper does not estimate. A single-jurisdiction deployment demonstrates the system but cannot supply its operational infrastructure. Comparable coalitions, including the Hague Apostille Convention, IBAN, SWIFT, ISO 20022, and eIDAS, took years to decades to form. The architecture supplies an object around which jurisdictions can coordinate without accelerating that political formation process.

17.2 Permanent features

Correctness non-verifiability. Sovereign signatures establish provenance. The system cannot distinguish a faithfully recorded incorrect attestation from a correct one. Audit rights, independent re-execution, cross-jurisdictional challenge, and reputational accountability remain the institutional safeguards for correctness. The architecture relies on these existing mechanisms.

Proposal-program epistemic limits. The compliance gate limits a program to (compliance-passing actions) \cap (delegated actions), including unwise actions within that intersection. Proposal outputs may be wrong. Scope gates on \delta, monetary caps within \delta, per-step gates for high-stakes workflows, principal oversight at discretion holes, and receipt-chain review constrain the resulting failure modes. Judgment risk remains.

Evaluator collusion. Cryptography cannot detect evaluators in different jurisdictions issuing consistent but incorrect attestations. External audit, independent re-execution, or whistleblowers must detect them, as they already detect some cross-jurisdictional fraud. Retaining each attestation and signer supports investigation after the fact. It does not prevent collusion.

Hosted-execution silent substitution. Execution identity relies on vendor attestation until hosted-execution providers publish verifiable runtime commitments. A provider may silently substitute a different runtime whose proposals pass admission but behave unexpectedly. Equivalence-class bindings narrow substitution’s scope, rationale records expose behavioral differences, and multi-provider execution reduces dependence on one provider in critical flows. These measures reduce the vulnerability while leaving residual risk.

Lawpack corruption at authoring. An adversary who corrupts a lawpack prior to its content-addressing produces signed-valid, substantively-wrong attestations thereafter. Lawpack integrity depends on the authoring jurisdiction’s processes.

17.3 Political preconditions

Race-to-the-bottom. The architecture lowers jurisdictional exit costs. Whether this produces quality-competition or laxity-competition is a political-economy question of participating jurisdictions. Remark 6.1 with Axiom 4.1 provides an algebraic floor on the in-scope fragment; corridor designs can mandate fresh evaluation of any domain the receiving jurisdiction does not trust. Fraudulent attestations and capture-at-source remain open to the same political-economy dynamics that drive current regulatory arbitrage.

Legitimacy of novel principals. Class IX’s participant-principal tree, Class X’s indigenous-council harbor, and multi-sovereign compacts require authority from the participants, communities, and sovereigns involved. Merkle-rooted trees, process-typed discretion holes, and multi-sovereign harbor sets can represent that authority. The architecture cannot create its legitimacy or prescribe the processes that establish it.

Policy environment. The architecture does not oppose transatlantic flows, substitute for the U.S. dollar, or bypass OFAC secondary-sanctions authority over participating parties. It makes compliance with applicable U.S.-led sanctions more auditable and supports enforcement of applicable EU frameworks wherever their reach is legitimate. The political-economic hypothesis is that cheap, verifiable compliance is positive-sum for many participants, including the United States and European Union, when they value auditability more than friction as leverage. Near-term corridors require aligned political will and corridor economics. The mechanism applies across corridors.

17.4 Engineering tradeoffs

Temporal drift. Bounded-asynchrony windows admit stale-snapshot actions. Monotone observation preserves in-flight consistency at the cost of postflight freeze when external events change the tensor.

Privacy. Zero-knowledge proofs cover predicates expressible as arithmetic circuits. Graduated disclosure with encrypted-at-rest supervisor-disclosure keys handles common regulatory cases. Many determinations rely on unstructured evidence without a clean cryptographic privacy regime.

Program-authoring governance. Governance determines quorum, timing, and veto discipline above \theta_\ast. The architecture fixes the immutability of \theta_\ast and its invariant set \mathcal{I}, from which Proposition 4.1 derives the reachability bound. Multi-signature quorums, time delays, and participant vetoes can compose above the gate.

Negotiation atomicity under adversarial failure. The companion Op paper’s session-typed commit gives local resource safety and bounded blocking under fail-stop assumptions. The Sovereign Jurisdiction Network paper states the finality-certificate and accountable-disagreement obligations for cross-zone atomicity. Their interaction under Byzantine-adversarial failure remains open.

17.4.1 Clearing allocation and identity preservation

A clearing witness must preserve the identities that give a payment its legal meaning: registered holder, economic beneficiary, legal estate, claim or tranche unit, quantity, purpose, remittance restriction, and the authority governing each field. Resource classes remain separate; operator income, service fees, customer funds, collateral, capital contributions, Fund entitlements, and distributions cannot be merged into one balance without losing ownership or restriction information.

For a closed batch of divisible claims in one settlement unit, let credits c have values v_c, obligations o have capacities U_o, and K_{co}\in\{0,1\} indicate that credit c may legally satisfy obligation o. The allocation problem is

\max\sum_{c,o}x_{co}\quad\text{subject to}\quad\sum_o x_{co}\le v_c,\quad\sum_c x_{co}\le U_o,\quad x_{co}=0\text{ if }K_{co}=0,\quad x_{co}\ge0.

The compatibility indicator includes beneficiary, purpose, remittance, estate, and already-committed-payment restrictions. It is not an asset-type predicate. The flow network joins a source to each credit with capacity v_c, joins a compatible credit to an obligation when K_{co}=1, and joins each obligation to the sink with capacity U_o; a source–sink cut in this network bounds every feasible allocation. A greedy allocator is not completion-complete: with v_a=v_b=U_x=U_y=1, K_{ax}=K_{ay}=K_{bx}=1, and K_{by}=0, choosing a\to x allocates one unit, while a\to y,b\to x allocates two. A feasible flow and a cut with equal value are an independently checkable certificate.

Proposition (bounded clearing allocation). In this closed, divisible, single-unit domain, a feasible flow whose value equals a matching cut bound allocates the maximum possible quantity.

Proof. Flow conservation gives a feasible allocation. The max-flow/min-cut theorem gives an upper bound equal to the cut capacity. Equality of the exhibited flow and cut proves optimality. This is a paper-level result; it is not production clearing or settlement evidence. \square

The construction does not cover online arrivals, indivisible or non-fungible property, nonlinear eligibility, already-final allocations, external performance, settlement finality, or recovery. Those extensions require canonical witness encodings, typed obstructions, provider and legal evidence, and a proof that receipt-chain and cross-asset commitments preserve the same identities. The full clearing layer remains open.

The principal open formal problems are corridor functoriality (full mixed-axis), proposal-program specification conformance, evaluator mechanism-design under incentive-compatibility, and withdrawal-propagation completeness on the derivative-use graph. The principal open specification is the clearing layer: matching, settlement (including binding a reliance class to a relying party), risk reservation, dispute, and recovery. Its inputs are the admissible holder set, reliance class, and clearing price of Definitions 4.6 to 4.8. This paper constructs the clearing layer’s typed inputs and does not construct the layer. No companion paper in this programme constructs the full layer either.

17.5 Failure modes and remedies

The following failure modes distinguish detection, loss bounds, and remedies. Bounds follow from scoped trust (Definition 5.3), delegation caps and rollback classes (§10), and fail-closed admission (§6). Detection uses receipt-chain replay (Definition 9.2), postflight attribution (Discipline 8.2), and institutional review (§17.2). Some mechanisms listed below remain design options: a standing replayer network, challenge protocol, evaluator bond, and rollback procedure. Their incentives belong to the open evaluator mechanism-design problem of §17.4.

  • Kernel bug admits a non-compliant action. Detection: the runtime fault handler, and replay of the receipt chain by any party holding it (Definition 9.2); a standing network of independent replayers is a design option. Loss bound: the admitted action’s settlement size, capped by the acting delegation’s monetary limits where the action ran under a delegation. Remedy: a compensating receipt reverting the action’s effects (Discipline 8.2), invalidation of the proofs that depended on it, lawpack or kernel refinement, and recovery flows for downstream operations; rollback of settled downstream state is a governance option. Insurance or indemnity may cover operational handling costs; proof/admission correctness itself remains outside indemnity.
  • Evaluator silently mis-signs an attestation. Detection: re-execution of the evaluation by an external auditor or a second jurisdiction, the institutional challenge of §17.2; a challenge protocol in which any party disputes an attestation by filing a structured counter-attestation is a design option over it. Loss bound: the affected asset’s exposure to the mis-signed domain; scoped trust (Definition 5.3) limits contagion to attestations from that evaluator under that lawpack for that domain within the validity window. Remedy: scoped trust restricts the blast radius; an evaluator bond posted at onboarding, paying verified loss, is a design option whose incentive-compatibility is the open problem of §17.4.
  • Lawpack corrupted at authoring. Detection: independent attestation of the lawpack at content-addressing, by the authoring sovereign’s legal department and a technical auditor, is a design option over the authoring jurisdiction’s processes; downstream consumers may challenge a lawpack. Loss: every attestation issued under the corrupted version is substantively wrong until detection. Remedy: lawpack republish, re-execution of affected evaluations, and recovery flows for any settled transaction whose verdict depended on the corruption.
  • Delegated program proposes a compliance-passing-but-substantively-unwise action. Loss bound: the program’s delegation monetary cap and per-window limits, differentiated by rollback class (§10.3): under a counter-signature-scoped delegation an unwise irreversible action cannot execute machine-solo, and the monetary caps bound the reversible and hard-to-reverse residue. Remedy: principal review at discretion holes; rationale review on the receipt chain admits forensic analysis in which each step of the stated plan is compared against the executed program range bound to it (§10.6); systematic patterns trigger amendment to the handle’s tool scope or the principal’s suspension of the delegation.
  • Sovereign revocation mid-settlement. The postflight gate catches external transitions and freezes the asset; in-flight settlements either complete under finality evidence, return a recorded obstruction, or compensate through the signed-commitment failure branch. Remedy: the compensation record is a first-class asset whose claim against the counterparty runs under the corridor’s agreed fallback rules.
  • Hosted-execution silent substitution. Detection: rationale-signature pattern analysis admits detection of behavioral drift; equivalence-class bindings (§10.1) reduce the substitution’s admissible scope. Loss bound: the program’s delegation monetary cap. Remedy: multi-provider execution for critical deployments; vendor-attestation audit.

Each failure’s loss scope depends on the delegation, attestation, and fail-closed rules, with recovery procedures named per failure mode.

18. Implications at Scale

The construction and its limits now permit a larger-scale question: under which adoption conditions do the lifecycles of §3 change investment, trade, and consent? The following mechanisms are conditional on their named preconditions and are not forecasts.

18.1 Investment attraction as a structural property

Consider asset classes excluded from allocator portfolios because verification costs are too high despite available returns. Trade-finance paper can yield three-to-four per cent over sovereign reference rates. Compliance-chain verification often excludes it from pension, insurance, and sovereign-wealth portfolios. Above fifty basis points of due diligence per receivable, an instrument may fall below an allocator’s net-of-fees floor and remain on bank balance sheets. Content-addressed chains, replayable evidence, and evaluator signatures under independently readable rules can reduce verification to signature checks, proof-bundle replay, and sampled re-execution. Standardizing this cost can lower the verification component of spreads only when legal, rating, performance, and mandate conditions also align.

The §3.7 pool shows how verification affects portfolio eligibility. Its Arabian-Chinese receivables are designed to become admissible to allocators previously limited to developed-market investment-grade credit. The risk team can re-execute the compliance projection, a rating agency can evaluate the chain instead of an issuer’s self-report, and Op’s signed commitment governs tranche settlement beyond bare counterparty trust. Infrastructure finance, consented scientific-corpus licensing, and jurisdictional-scale carbon stewardship offer analogous cases when verification cost is the bottleneck. The potential inflow is existing capital excluded by that cost.

Lower verification cost connects existing investable yield with capital that may be authorized to buy it. The mechanism requires neither a new return premium nor a promised cash flow.

18.2 Trade-volume expansion as receivables-financing unlock

A bilateral corridor’s trade growth may be limited by demand, capital, policy, or infrastructure. In the demand-limited case, the real economy cannot produce or consume more. In the capital-limited case, it can, but financing cannot support the additional trade. Arabian-Chinese trade, Association of Southeast Asian Nations-China trade, Belt-and-Road infrastructure finance, and Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership inter-member flows are candidates for the latter diagnosis, not assumed examples. The test has three parts: exporters could ship more but cannot finance the working-capital gap, buyers could order more but cannot extend payment on bounded-cost terms, and verification costs exclude capital that could finance the gap (§18.1).

Section 3.7 supplies the mechanism: originate receivables as intelligent assets, aggregate them under published pool methodologies, and admit institutional capital through Op’s signed commitment protocol. This can increase trade only where verification time and cost bind financing. A receivable turning in two days rather than forty-five can finance twenty times as many shipments from the same capital before other constraints bind. Whether real-economy capacity then becomes the constraint is an empirical question.

Receivables financing is a plausible near-term source of Arabian-Chinese trade growth only if capital, rather than demand, constrains the corridor. Infrastructure finance is a medium-term complement, while consented-data and scientific-exchange flows are separate long-term categories. Demand, the political preconditions of bilateral trade, and each party’s export-control and sanctions authority remain external to the mechanism. It addresses stalled capital-gated growth. Where demand binds, receivables financing is not the active constraint, although other intelligent-asset mechanisms may matter.

Corridor-level analysis must determine whether the same mechanism applies to Korea-China, Japan-China, ASEAN-China bilateral and plurilateral flows, and Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership inter-member flows. Other candidates are the cross-border components of Eurasian Economic Union and African Continental Free Trade Area flows, and Belt-and-Road infrastructure-finance relationships.

18.3 Methodology-level compounding

A population of assets can also improve the methodology under which it operates. Examples include voluntary-carbon methodologies, programmable-claims standards, treasury-operations frameworks, and trade-receivables-pool methodologies. Each asset contributes observations to the methodology’s world model through the mechanisms in the compounding-intelligence companion paper. A larger observation base can improve anomaly detection, ground precedent retrieval in evidence, and let authors test proposed rules against the empirical distribution of admitted assets. Under adoption and data-quality assumptions, more observations can improve methodologies, asset ratings, and access to investable capital, supporting further adoption. Section 10.4’s proposer/admission-filter discipline routes proposal-program observations into that world model through a sanitized channel that preserves their information content and epistemic separation.

18.4 Trust at population scale

The participant-principal tree (§14) reduces the number of consent relationships by introducing shared infrastructure. Without it, N participants and M relying parties, such as licensees, downstream researchers, and buyers of derived products, maintain on the order of N \times M relationships. A withdrawal or changed use requires renegotiation. Under the tree, participants each consent once and relying parties each rely once on the composed cells, giving on the order of N + M relationships. Withdrawal propagates through the derivative-use graph in place of M renegotiations. This changes feasibility for a two-million-participant genomic consortium, ten-million-member platform cooperative, population-scale basic-income experiment, or nation-scale electronic-identity system. The reduction from N \times M to N + M can make such systems workable if identity, delegation, withdrawal, and dispute procedures are accepted. The Merkle-rooted tree, structured-consent cells, and derivative-use propagation make consent an asset property at scales that strain bilateral legal arrangements.

18.5 Corridor-network equilibrium

In a mature network with mutual recognition across dozens of harbor pairs, institutions face four distinct costs: local-only operation, cross-harbor meet constraints, corridor friction, and non-computational switching. Weak floors can let laxity attract local-only operations. A stricter harbor still in the meet continues to bind a cross-harbor operation. Jurisdictions therefore keep negotiating corridor formation, mutual recognition, and domains of retained sovereignty. The architecture supplies a shared object for these negotiations without determining their outcome. Whether the network evolves toward quality or laxity remains the political-economy question of §17.

18.6 What follows without preconditions

All implications in §18.1-18.5 require §17’s preconditions: legal recognition of attestations, participating jurisdictions, operational evaluators, bilateral corridor agreements, established methodologies, and political willingness to admit receipt chains as evidence. The architecture supplies the object around which deployment can form. Deployment itself requires political and economic action. Where the preconditions hold, the mechanisms operate. Where they fail, those implications do not follow.

19. Conclusion

An intelligent asset combines its founding record, claim program, lifecycle programs, sovereign-attested compliance state, append-only history, and persistent store. The lifecycle programs let it observe, plan, and act. Its compliance function combines attestations by pointwise meet where applicability agrees and by named-obstruction reduction elsewhere, while a labeled DAG propagates changes. The claim program extends contract combinators with authority gates and typed discretion holes. Its denotation under the composed verdict determines admissible holders, reliance, and clearing price. The invariant set and amendment gate fixed at genesis constrain every admitted amendment chain. SAVM executes the compliance-gated state transitions. Principal-carried authorization supplies procedurally structured human judgments at discretion holes, supervisory orders take precedence over private delegation, and participant-principal trees extend consent to populations.

The same record supports financial instruments, multi-jurisdictional treasuries, physical infrastructure, consent-governed scientific corpora, and stewarded natural systems. Class-specific fields supply the differing consent structures, custody graphs, and settlement milestones.

The construction makes compliance an asset property, composes it algebraically across jurisdictions, and preserves a cryptographically auditable history. Its guarantees concern provenance, forensic observability, and bounded delegated action. Evaluator correctness and collusion prevention still depend on institutions, legal force on external recognition, coalition formation on politics, and substantive judgment on principals. Typed decision points and supervisory authority preserve the boundary between machine computation and procedurally structured human judgment. Shared consent infrastructure can support populations beyond the reach of bilateral relationships under the stated acceptance conditions.

Section 3’s lifecycles motivate the construction in §§4-14. Section 17 states the limits governing both that construction and its implications.
