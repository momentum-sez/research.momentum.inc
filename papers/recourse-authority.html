Recourse: A Decentralized Network of AI Courts

23 November 2026 · Raeez Lorgat · Momentum Research · raeez@momentum.inc · PDF

Abstract

Companies, governments and people increasingly act through AI agents, whose disputes still wait on lawyers and banks. A model can answer a dispute in minutes, yet the loser never agreed to be bound by a model, cannot test reasons that a model writes as persuasively for either result, and has no person to hold responsible. The winner is still unpaid, and the agents will apply the same rule again.

We introduce Recourse, a decentralized network of AI courts, which runs dispute resolution and carries outcomes into execution. Under the parties’ agreement, several models, each working alone, compute an AI court’s verdict from the record of the case and from precedent, in findings that each cite evidence and authority anyone can look up. A lawyer checks them, decides where the models divide, signs and answers for them, and whoever would decide otherwise owes an argument of the same kind. Each later step is recorded so that each institution asked to act on the decision can check it first, and the agents’ systems can learn from the finished case.

1. Introduction

Consider two companies that run their routine business through AI agents. Asha’s company stores frozen food in two cold rooms, and Kofi’s maintains the compressor that serves each. Kofi’s agent schedules engineers and issues invoices; Asha’s agent checks invoices and pays them. Kofi’s engineer overhauls both compressors and signs a service report. Kofi’s agent invoices US$40,000 the same day. Two days later one cold room is warm again, and Asha’s agent withholds payment. Kofi wants his invoice paid. Asha wants to pay only for work that held.

A capable model, given the contract and the service report, produces an answer in minutes. The difficulty is what turns an answer into a decision. Suppose the model says that Asha owes US$30,000. She is free to ignore it, since she never agreed that a model should decide. She can fairly ask why she should trust reasons that the same model would have written as persuasively for the opposite result [12], and who will answer to her if they are wrong. Even if she accepts the answer, Kofi is still unpaid, because the money is with her bank, which acts only on its own authority.

Existing arrangements meet parts of this. Arbitration lets the parties choose who decides, and expedited rules shorten the procedure [5], but the decision waits on a person’s time. An online marketplace settles tens of millions of its customers’ disputes a year [11], but only under that company’s terms. An arbitral institution has offered an AI arbitrator whose draft award a human arbitrator reviews [11], but a person handed a machine’s draft cannot check it without redoing the work [17].

We introduce Recourse, a decentralized network of AI courts, which runs dispute resolution and carries outcomes into execution. An AI court is a dispute-resolution centre whose decisions are made by AI, under the mandate that the parties’ agreement and the procedure they adopted confer. The network is decentralized in that a court, a custodian or a bank acts on another’s decision only after its own check of the authority and the evidence behind it. Under assumptions stated in Section 8, anyone given an institution’s record can trace each action in it to the authority and evidence it relied on.

This paper builds on a companion research paper, the formal Recourse paper [1], and adds how an AI court decides. The sections follow Kofi’s claim from filing to payment (Figure 1).

2. Who may decide

A decision binds the side that disagrees with it, so whoever decides needs authority over both sides. In arbitration that authority comes from the parties’ agreement, and what parties may agree is wide: who decides, under which law, by what procedure. An agreement that a machine compute the decision is one more such choice, as Sir Geoffrey Vos observed of arbitration when Master of the Rolls of England and Wales [11]. What an AI court issues under such an agreement is not an arbitral award. The convention under which courts enforce awards across borders applies to awards [2], so abroad the court’s decision binds with the force of a contract.

Asha and Kofi made such an agreement. Their maintenance contract has an arbitration clause that names an AI court in the country whose law governs the contract and gives that court a mandate to determine disputes about the work. The contract also places US$10,000 of Asha’s money with a custodian as a retention, to be released on the court’s decision. The court will keep the agreement in its record of the case, byte for byte, with the parties and the claims it covers, so that anyone who later relies on the decision can check that the clause covers the dispute.

Kofi’s agent files the claim where it files every claim, with the dispute-resolution centre that his company uses in its country, which we call Kofi’s centre. A centre takes a claim in first, preserving it with its source material, and settles afterwards who may decide it. This filing names the parties, sets out the facts, asks for payment of US$40,000 and gives the arbitration clause as its basis of jurisdiction. The centre records all of it as what Kofi asserts.

Kofi’s centre, whether or not it is itself an AI court, has no mandate over this dispute and refers the case to the court that has one. Kofi permits his file to be disclosed to the AI court, his centre authorizes the referral, and the AI court accepts the matter. Kofi’s centre keeps its case open, since the AI court’s mandate is only to decide and the decision will come back to be carried out. The AI court opens a case of its own by an explicit filing in its record and establishes jurisdiction, procedure and appointments for its acts.

3. The record of the case

The court will decide on what the two sides put before it, and each side speaks through a lawyer. Kofi finds his in a directory. She will file his case over her signature. A valid signature can accompany a false statement or an act outside its signer’s powers. Kofi’s engineer can sign a report that is wrong, and a lawyer can sign a filing for a client who never engaged her. Checking a signature is authentication, which establishes who signed. Authority is a different question, of which actor may take which action, for which claims and during which period, by virtue of an agreement, an appointment, a delegation or a legal power. So before Kofi’s lawyer files anything, the court has on record the engagement he made with her, once conflicts were cleared, and the mandate that names the claims and actions he entrusted to her (Appendix A.4). It accepts a filing from her only within that mandate. Asha’s lawyer answers on the same footing.

What Kofi’s lawyer files is a package: the relief requested, a chronology, the source documents, the facts in dispute, the relevant legal authorities and the questions of evidence that remain open. The source documents are the contract, the service report, the invoice and the messages between the two companies. Much of what happened was done by the two agents, so the package also holds their operating records, among them the temperature log that Asha’s agent keeps. An AI agent can do much of the assembling under the lawyer’s delegation. She reviews the package before it is filed.

The court computes from the bytes of each document it receives a value that identifies them, called a digest, and keeps the two together in its record. If the two sides file different versions of one document, the record holds both, each under its digest. Which to believe is a question for the decision. The record is closed before anything is computed, once each side holds a receipt for what it filed and can see what the other filed.

4. How an AI court decides

4.1 The verdict

Before any person decides anything, the court computes a verdict (Figure 2). The models that compute it are given the record of the case, the contract with the procedure the parties adopted in it, the law that governs the contract, and precedent. They may rely on nothing else. Precedent means the judgments that bind under the governing law, as they bind any arbitrator applying it, and the court’s earlier decisions (Section 6).

A verdict answers a list of questions, drawn by the court’s administrator from the claim’s terms and from each side’s case. Both sides see the list before any model runs and may contest it (Appendix A.5). Kofi’s claim turns on clause 7 of the contract, which prices each compressor at US$10,000 for parts, payable when fitted, and US$10,000 for labour, payable when the room it serves has held −18 °C for seven days. Kofi’s case is that the work was done and all four sums are due. Asha’s is that she pays only for work that held.

An answer to such a question has to be one that the losing side can test. Asha’s objection to a model’s answer was that its reasons could have been written as persuasively for the opposite result. The test of a conclusion is therefore whether it rests on a document and an authority that anyone can look up. So the answers are findings. Each names the evidence and the authority it rests on and quotes the passage it relies on. A finding of record says what an identified document says or what a side admits, and anyone can compute it again from the document; whether the document tells the truth is a question for a finding of judgment [1, §5]. A finding of judgment says whether a contested fact is proved, what a term means or what the law requires. A derived finding is computed from the others by the claim’s terms, and the sum owed is always derived. Kofi’s contract mandates the court to determine disputes about the work, so here findings of judgment decide his whole claim:

  1. Record. The service report, signed by the engineer and recorded on the day of the work, says that parts were fitted to both compressors (the report).
  2. Record. Asha does not dispute that the parts were fitted (her answer).
  3. Record. The temperature log records cold room 1 at −18 °C or below throughout the seven days after the overhaul (the log).
  4. Record. The log records cold room 2 above −18 °C from the second day (the log).
  5. Judgment. Parts are payable on fitting, whatever the rooms did afterwards (clause 7).
  6. Judgment. Labour is earned compressor by compressor (clause 7; the governing law on conditions of payment).
  7. Judgment. Labour not yet earned stays payable if its room later holds −18 °C for seven days. Clause 7 fixes no last date (clause 7).
  8. Derived. Asha owes US$20,000 for parts and US$10,000 for labour on the first compressor, US$30,000 in all. A further US$10,000 is conditional (findings 1 to 7; the invoice).

One model’s answer to a question of judgment may owe more to its training than to the record. The court therefore puts each such question to a panel of several models, trained separately by different makers and none on another’s output. Each model answers alone, a stated number of times, and says whether a reasonable decision-maker could reach the other answer on this record; a model that says so, or whose repeated answers differ, doubts. The court publishes a quorum of more than half the panel, two of the three in this example. A question is agreed when every model gives the same answer and none doubts. It is unsettled when a quorum gives the same answer and another model differs or doubts; the verdict then takes the quorum’s answer and records the other reading beside it, with its passage. Without a quorum the question is unresolved. The verdict then makes no finding and gives the result under each reading. Here the panel agrees on findings 5 and 7 and is unsettled on finding 6. One model reads clause 7 as making all labour wait until both rooms hold, which would leave US$20,000 due.

Agreement removes what is peculiar to one model and leaves what the models share, such as knowing some legal systems far better than others [11]. A finding on a point of law must therefore cite an authority of the law the court applies. Where none can be cited the question is unresolved and left to a person, so that a thinly documented legal system is not decided by what the models know of another.

The court’s record of the decision keeps what can be inspected afterwards: which models and versions computed, exactly what each was given, and each one’s separate answers.

The findings on the temperature log say what the log records. Since neither side says the log is wrong, clause 7 is applied to what it records. If the log in the record were not the log that was kept, findings 3 and 4 would have to be computed again, and with them every finding that rests on them.

4.2 The practitioner who signs

Before the verdict binds anyone it goes to the court’s certifying practitioner. She is a lawyer admitted to practise the law the court applies, accredited under published criteria by a body the adopted procedure names, insured, unconnected with either party and distinct from the lawyers who represent them. The court’s appointing authority has drawn her for this case from those accredited and free of conflicts. Her pay is a fixed sum, set by contract before any assignment and reserved in full by the court. Outcomes, recovery, hours, workload, appeals and the number of assignments cannot increase it [1, §10], so her pay gives her nothing to gain from how the case comes out or how long it lasts (Appendix A.8).

Her signature certifies stated checks: that each item cited is in the record under its digest and says what the finding says it says; that each authority exists and holds what it is cited for; that every point either side made is answered by some finding; and that the result follows. Where the models agreed, these checks are her whole task. Where they did not, she decides the question herself, starting from the verdict. To keep the quorum’s answer she writes a sentence, with a passage, saying why the other reading fails; to leave it she owes the argument that Section 4.3 describes. Both sides receive the verdict in draft and may object within a short fixed time. She decides every objection and every matter reserved to a person.

The parties’ lawyers review packages that agents largely assemble. The certifying practitioner neither prepares the case nor decides it afresh, as the reviewer of a machine’s draft award must to check it. Her work is bounded by the findings, the passages and authorities they cite, and the two sides’ objections. The two sides know their files and have the motive to find what is missing; she decides what they raise.

On Kofi’s claim neither side objects to a finding. She confirms the findings of record against the report, Asha’s answer and the log, reads clause 7 against findings 5 and 7, and decides finding 6 herself. She keeps it, on the ground that clause 7 ties each labour price to “the room it serves”, so each is earned on its own room.

When she signs, the verdict becomes the AI court’s determination. Her signature binds it to the identity and provenance of the evidence it relied on; if either is later altered, a new signature is required. She answers to the two sides for the care with which she did what her certificate says she did: the checks, her decisions, her declaration that she has no conflict, and the form of any departure. A tribunal’s deciding a point differently does not by that alone show want of care, and a careless decision of hers is not excused because the finding has some support.

4.3 A person who would decide otherwise

The practitioner may refuse to sign what the models computed; otherwise the machine would be the judge and her signature a formality. But if she could put her view in the verdict’s place unexplained, the parties would lose what the verdict gave them, which is reasons they can check. A person who would decide otherwise must give a satisfactory argument for the new result, one that can be checked as a finding can.

An argument that displaces a finding of judgment is a departure. It names the finding and gives the new answer, states its ground, quotes the record exactly wherever it relies on it, and states the result, which must equal what the findings then yield when computed again. The grounds are evidence that is new, wrongly bound or wrongly read; an authority missed or misapplied, including one outside the court’s collection; a question missing from the list; a party not heard on a point; or a matter that the law or the procedure reserves to human judgment.

The court accepts a departure as its decision only in this form, though it keeps whatever is submitted. A program checks the form and nothing else. No model is asked whether an argument persuades it. Whether the argument is right is for people. The two sides receive any departure that changes what a side gets before it takes effect, with a short time to object. Where the practitioner leaves an answer that a quorum of the models gave and the sum now payable changes, a second practitioner, accredited and appointed as she was, must countersign before the determination issues; if the second will not, the question goes to a tribunal.

Suppose she had thought US$25,000 a fairer figure. The court would not have accepted the change, since no finding yields that sum and no rule reserves it to her. Nor could she have taken the other reading of clause 7 on her signature alone. That would have been a departure, naming finding 6, the passage read the other way and the US$20,000 that follows. It would have gone to both sides and to a second practitioner before it took effect.

Kofi’s case has one matter that the procedure reserves to human judgment. Asha’s lawyer has asked for time, because Asha’s cash is tied up in stock until her customers pay. Whether to give a debtor time turns on no fact or rule in the record. It is a discretion, which people entrust to a person because it calls for mercy as well as judgment. On a reserved matter the models make no finding, and the verdict states a default in its place: here, that the US$30,000 is payable at once. Kofi is shown the request. The certifying practitioner allows thirty days for the US$20,000 that the retention does not cover, on condition that Asha lodges her signed payment instruction now. For those thirty days that sum is unsecured. Her decision records the rule that reserves the matter and the default it displaces, the facts she considered, that Kofi was heard, the security he has meanwhile, and her reasons.

She signs. Under the determination Asha owes US$30,000, US$20,000 of it in thirty days. The fourth US$10,000 is a conditional amount whose condition has not been met. It is not yet due, and the determination does not end it. If cold room 2 later holds for seven days, the log that shows it is performance of the condition and no new dispute.

4.4 What the determination is worth

The determination binds Asha and Kofi as their contract provides (Section 2), with the effect that the applicable law gives such an agreement. The formal Recourse paper describes a determination that no one takes to a tribunal as binding contractually in the manner of an expert determination [1, §6]. Contractual force carries the determination into performance wherever performance is voluntary or already arranged, as the custodian’s mandate arranges the release of the retention.

Either party can require a tribunal of people, appointed as the arbitration agreement provides, without giving reasons or obtaining leave. A party may need an award, and a party about to lose should be able to put its case to a person before the loss is final [16]. The procedure fixes the period for asking: in this example, fourteen days from completed service of the certified determination, with access to the full record.

A request names the findings it contests and reaches only the items of the claim whose sums depend on them, as far as those items can be separated. On those items the determination ceases to bind and remains as evidence [1, §6], and whatever has not been performed on them is suspended. Kofi could ask for the fourth US$10,000 by contesting finding 4, and the three sums already determined would stand. The tribunal starts from the verdict and its record. It hears the parties on the findings they contest, checks the rest, and issues an award with its reasons, adopting the verdict or departing from it. If no one requires a tribunal in time, the determination stands, open only to a challenge of the kind Section 5.3 describes. Neither Asha nor Kofi requires one.

5. From decision to payment

The determination says that Asha owes Kofi US$30,000. Paying him is the work of other institutions, each acting on its own authority.

5.1 Acceptance

The AI court’s mandate to decide ends with the determination. The court remains the keeper of the determination’s status: it records requests for a tribunal, challenges, stays and corrections to it and answers what now restricts it. Carrying the determination out falls to Kofi’s centre, where the claim was filed and the case stayed open. To that centre the determination is a report of what another institution did. It arrives with its exact content, the authority of its source, its signatures, the steps it depends on and the scope of permitted disclosure. The centre checks these against keys and rules that it configured itself.

It accepts the determination into its record only if the determination addresses the current revision of the centre’s case, the AI court’s mandate covers this decision and these claims, the signed content is the content received, the steps that had to come first were taken, and no challenge, stay or later decision bars it (Appendix A.1). Every institution in the network accepts every action into its record on these terms and decides for itself; the design assumes no global clock, shared database or authority above its members. Each record is built from signed events, retained histories and conditional changes of state [7, §4].

5.2 Instructions

The money is with a custodian and a bank. The custodian releases the US$10,000 retention under its mandate. Asha’s bank pays US$20,000 in thirty days on the signed instruction that she lodged; without her instruction, execution would need a court’s authority.

Kofi’s centre sends both instructions. For each it makes a record that fixes the obligation, the destination, the exact action and its legal basis. It records the instruction durably before sending it, with its exact bytes bound to a stable identity, so that the instruction sent is the instruction recorded. The determination that the centre holds is a snapshot. Between recording and sending, authority can lapse and a restriction can arrive. The centre therefore asks the AI court what now restricts the determination, and the answer states the time up to which it is complete. It checks again at the moment it sends.

The release instruction passes. The custodian checks the certified determination against its mandate and releases the US$10,000, and Kofi’s bank reports that it has arrived.

5.3 A challenge and a stay

Before the thirty days are up, Asha challenges one item of evidence. She accepts the reading of the contract and does not require a tribunal. The temperature log in the record, she says, is not the log her agent kept: the entries for cold room 1 were changed after it was filed. Her challenge names finding 3, gives its ground, evidence wrongly bound, and says what would follow, US$20,000 owed. It goes to the court’s challenge authority, a practitioner who took no part in the determination, is cleared of conflicts and acts under her own mandate.

The challenge authority orders a stay. Until the challenge is decided, nothing more may be sent under the determination. The stay enters the AI court’s record. It stops a payment only through the record of the institution that sends the payment. On the due date Kofi’s centre asks the AI court what now restricts the determination, learns of the stay and enters it in its record, where the check at the moment of sending finds it. A stay that arrived after the instruction had left would not have stopped it: an instruction that has left can be controlled only where it was sent, and a stay after an act changes only what is permitted from then on. The US$10,000 already released therefore stays with Kofi.

The challenge authority compares the bytes of the log with the digest recorded when the log was filed. They match. A match shows that these are the bytes that were filed. A claim that the log was false from the start would be a question of fact for a finding of judgment. Here the challenge is dismissed and joins the record as an allegation that failed. The order lifting the stay names the stay it lifts. A challenge that succeeds produces a corrected determination, which replaces the first and names it.

5.4 Payment

With the stay lifted, the payment instruction passes its check and Kofi’s centre presents it to Asha’s bank. No confirmation comes back before the connection times out. A timeout can follow a successful payment, so the centre records the instruction as attempted, with its result unknown, and does not send it again merely because confirmation failed.

Inside the network a repeated request is harmless. An institution that receives an identical request again, from an actor entitled to make it, returns the result it recorded the first time. It can do so because it stored the action, the state the action produced and the answer to a repeat together or not at all. Because a bank owes no such promise, a repeat could pay Kofi twice. A replacement requires evidence that everything the original has already delivered is accounted for at Kofi’s bank and that it can deliver nothing further (Appendix A.7). A report that no payment has been seen is not that evidence. The centre waits, which prevents a second or an unauthorized payment and delays completion.

Evidence from the receiving end settles the matter. Some days later Kofi’s bank reports a credit of US$20,000. A credit is an entry in the recipient’s account at the recipient’s bank. Matching the credit to the debt it pays is itself an act that needs authority. The custodian’s release, matched earlier, had left US$20,000 outstanding. This credit brings the balance to zero.

Kofi has not yet collected. A credit that has been reported, funds that Kofi can use and value that he has collected are three states. Collection requires evidence from the recipient, legal finality of the transfer, and the closing of every period in which the bank or the law still permits a reversal. When the last such period closes, Kofi has collected US$30,000.

6. What the case teaches

Kofi has been paid, but his agent and Asha’s will apply clause 7 again at the next overhaul. Two parties with opposite interests have examined the same events, and a decision with reasons now says how the rule applied. The finished case is kept in a form that can be learned from, with its evidence, the precedent relied on, the verdict, the practitioner’s reasons, the determination and what was paid.

Within the AI court the case becomes precedent. The court’s earlier decisions have no force as law. It follows them, where it is permitted to use them and both sides of the later case can read them, so that like cases are decided alike, and a verdict that departs from one says why. Later verdicts in like cases cite its findings and any argument by which a person departed from the verdict: the practitioner’s reasons for giving Asha time are there the next time a debtor asks.

The case also returns to where the dispute began, because both agents followed their procedures into it. Kofi’s agent demanded US$10,000 of labour seven days before it was due and another US$10,000 that has not become due. Asha’s agent withheld US$20,000 for parts that was due whatever the cold rooms did.

The two agents operate in Mass, a decentralized network of sovereign execution environments for AI agents, separate from Recourse and connected to it by an interface. An environment checks each operation its agent proposes against the rules of the authority it belongs to and records what was done. Mass is told the outcome so that the two companies’ operations can respond. Each Mass environment learns the correct, safe interpretation of the law, policy and regulation that apply to it, and so keeps pace with fraud and bad actors on its own, through an institutional world model whose weights are trained on rulings, investigations and completed work [18]. A determination is such a ruling. The case behind it shows which rule governed the operation, which procedure admitted it and what went wrong. Fraud is the main learning priority. It is not in issue between Asha and Kofi.

Each environment takes from the case a candidate procedure: to demand labour when its room’s test has run, and to pay what no reading of the record puts in doubt and withhold only the disputed item. Each tests its candidate under its own mandate before adopting it. The environments learn which reading of clauses like clause 7 holds and which procedure leads to fewer disputes.

What passes back is a record naming the deciding authority, the supporting evidence, the version of the rule applied and the use permitted. Learning from it needs the permission of those whose records these are, or of the law.

7. A claim with no agreement

Kofi’s contract gave the court a mandate to decide his claim. Mira’s digital assets were stolen. She has no contract with the thief and does not know who the thief is. The transaction history shows where the assets went and leaves open who controls the addresses that received them, whether Mira owned what was taken, which court has jurisdiction and what remedy is available. Only the law can bind a party who has agreed to nothing, and the law acts through the court that has jurisdiction. Until evidence connects the receiving addresses to persons, the record keeps the defendant’s identity explicitly unknown. To proceed against an unidentified defendant, to give notice of the proceeding by an alternative method, to protect the assets meanwhile, and to have a judgment recognized and executed elsewhere each require their own basis in law, which membership of the network does not supply.

The court that has jurisdiction decides Mira’s claim, and an AI court does much of the work on it. It preserves the claim and the evidence, connects her with a lawyer, and prepares the case under that lawyer’s review as Section 3 describes. From the closed record the models compute an assessment, in findings that cite the record and the law of the court that will decide. The assessment binds no one. Where the court’s law permits, Mira’s lawyer files it with the case as a report served on both sides, so that the judge can see which findings each side contests. What the judge does with the report is for the court’s law, and the judgment is the judge’s. A judge’s decision cannot pass to a machine unless that law is changed [11, 14].

8. What the record guarantees

Each institution in Kofi’s case accepted every action into its record only on the terms of Section 5.1. What that guarantees rests on assumptions: that signatures authenticate the messages they sign; that content digests resist substitution; that each institution maintains its trusted keys, its records of who may do what and its adopted rules; that local transactions store together the revision of the case, the accepted action and the answer to a repeated request; that validity periods and deadlines use a trustworthy local clock; and that identifiers go on referring to the same parties, claims, documents and economic effects.

Under these assumptions, every action in a record identifies its predecessor and passed the checks for its type when it was accepted. The argument is an induction (Appendix A.9). An action is accepted only if it names the current state as its predecessor and passes its checks against that state, and the action and the state it produces are stored together. Anyone given the history can therefore verify a record by replaying it, without sending another instruction to a bank.

Consent, jurisdiction, procedural fairness, recognition [9] and execution are determined under the applicable law, and the record carries each such determination with its authority. Of a verdict the record shows that each quoted passage is in the document cited, that the result can be computed again from the findings, and that a named person certified it.

For awards made by tribunals, the formal Recourse paper proves that the recorded history keeps awards, permissions to act and payments distinct through later stays, corrected reports and repeated evidence, and that no payment is counted twice across claims [1, §§9.6–9.7]. A determination never enters that model’s set of awards, whoever countersigns it.

Appendix A. Records and rules in detail

This appendix gives the admission rule as a formula, the full contents of the records and the remaining rules.

A.1 The admission rule as a formula (Section 5.1)

For institution i, let S denote the recorded state of the case and a a requested action. Admission means accepting that action into the institution’s record. We specify:

\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Admit}_i(S,a) ={}&\operatorname{Version}_i(S,a) \land\operatorname{Authority}_i(S,a)\\ &\land\operatorname{Evidence}_i(S,a) \land\operatorname{Procedure}_i(S,a) \land\operatorname{Restrictions}_i(S,a). \end{aligned}

The five conjuncts are the checks that Section 5.1 shows on one act. Version: the action names the revision of the case it was made against, and that revision is still current, so two incompatible changes cannot both be admitted against the same state. Authority: the actor’s mandate covers this action and these claims and is in force. Evidence: the documents and records the action relies on are bound to it. Procedure: the steps that must come before the action have been taken. Restrictions: no challenge, suspension or superseding decision bars it. Admission depends on the type of the action and on its legal route, such as an arbitration or a court proceeding: a stay of execution bars a payment instruction and leaves a party free to file a challenge or further evidence. An authorized repeat with identical content returns the result recorded for that request identity, and changed content requires a new admissible action, which must pass the checks itself.

What the checks must survive. Participants can submit false evidence, exceed the powers delegated to them, conceal a later event or repeat a message. A message can be lost after its recipient has acted. AI can omit relevant material, invent a citation, or take an instruction embedded in a piece of evidence for a direction it is authorized to follow.

Signed actions. A signed action binds its purpose, the institution, the case, its payload and the state that preceded it. Expected revisions prevent incompatible accepted changes against the same local state. The design requires atomic storage: the accepted action, the resulting state and the response to a repeated request commit together.

A.2 The network and the systems it connects to (Sections 5.1 and 6)

The centre and the network. The network connects dispute-resolution centres, practitioners and the systems that hold records or assets, and it connects preparation, proceedings, review and execution through records that identify the authority required for each action. A dispute-resolution centre administers proceedings under the authority it holds and the rules it has adopted. It maintains the case records, the appointments, the disclosure permissions and the service commitments. Shared interfaces support the discovery of practitioners, referrals and the exchange of authenticated outcomes. Execution coordination carries eligible outcomes and exact instructions to the actors entitled to perform them, and those actors return evidence of what occurred.

Actors. The actors in a case include the parties, their representatives, software agents, administrators, decision-makers, reviewers and the institutions that carry out what is decided. Figure 3 shows the institutions and what passes between them.

Evidence across institutions. The evidence, the professional work and the power to carry out a decision sit with different institutions. Someone must establish what happened, someone entitled to decide must decide, and when a decision orders payment someone must still identify the account entitled to receive it, authorize the payment and establish what arrived. Practitioners must reconstruct events from contracts, messages, registries and transaction records, and each institution needs the evidence that bears on its own responsibility. A record that passes between institutions must carry its permitted use, the authority that governs it and any later correction, because the institution that receives it will act on it.

Receiving institutions. A receiving institution establishes its own basis for accepting a case, relying on an outcome or requesting action. Common record formats reduce the work of interpretation without transferring institutional powers.

Courts and service contracts. Sovereign courts remain institutions owned by their country, whatever technology and support they obtain under contract. Their constituting law governs judicial power, appointments and administration. A contract with a service company can specify technology, support and service payments, and it conveys only the rights it grants. The same separation governs arbitration centres and independent providers.

Participation terms. Participation terms specify the relationships they cover, the disclosures permitted, the procedures and the service duties. Qualified people and funded capacity perform the work. Two separate participation relationships create no additional relationship merely because they share a participant.

Mass. Mass is a decentralized network of sovereign execution environments for AI agents. Each environment determines whether operations may run under its governing authority’s rules and records their results. Cases concerning company operations require records of the relevant acts, and the interface with Mass supplies permitted operating records to Recourse and accepts competent outcomes for the corresponding operational response: Asha’s agent, which withheld the payment, then has a competent outcome to act on. A record that crosses the interface keeps its acting authority, its rule version, its evidence and its disclosure permission.

Moxie. Moxie prices and clears claims. An economic claim records a right or an obligation and the conditions that determine its outcome, and Kofi’s invoice is such a claim. The interface with Moxie identifies the economic claim whose pricing or clearing depends on a dispute and supplies its status for those operations. It carries that claim’s identity, its restrictions and the outcome, and it preserves the distinction between an asserted entitlement and an established right. Moxie receives the outcome of Kofi’s case, so that anyone pricing his invoice, or taking it from him by assignment, knows that the amount awaited from the court is fixed at US$30,000, with the US$40,000 kept on the record as what was asserted. Mass and Moxie perform complementary functions, and records from either system enter Recourse under the same evidence requirements as other records.

Kofi’s claim at this interface. What is in dispute is an amount of money that has not been fixed. His filed claim is recorded as an amount awaited from the named court, with the US$40,000 beside it as what was asserted. The determination fixes the amount at US$30,000, item by item, and the amount of an item on which a tribunal is required is awaited again, from the tribunal. A stay restricts the sending of instructions and changes no amount. Execution is by instructions under the rules of Appendix A.7, and recovery is entries at the recipient’s bank. A successful challenge is a linked correction. A funder’s share is a holding in the claim, or a separate claim on the claimant. A debt whose amount is already fixed, and that is filed only to be collected, is not an amount awaited, and a proceeding that asks for something other than money is not an amount at all.

Economic claims and legal claims. An economic claim can concern money, assets, ownership, delivery, performance or events. A legal claim can contest an economic right, seek its performance or concern conduct outside an existing contract. The two records can refer to each other, and each keeps its own meaning.

Other asset systems. Typed references, which state the kind of object they point to, identify Mass and Moxie objects. Assets held in systems other than Mass and Moxie require their own identifiers, custody evidence and execution contracts, and a document that describes an external asset supplies neither its native identifier nor an execution contract.

Transfers of economic rights. Because the two kinds of claim are separate records, a transfer of one is not a transfer of the other. If Kofi assigns his invoice, the assignment requires its own legal basis and changes only the rights that its legal instrument transfers. Representation of the case, control of the proceedings and rights of appointment remain governed by their own instruments and require separate authority. The same distinction applies when a legal claim produces an economic right that can be transferred.

A.3 Jurisdiction, referral and the court route (Sections 2 and 7)

Claims and cases. A legal claim asserts entitlement to a remedy or a determination, and its record identifies the parties, the basis asserted, the relevant facts and the relief requested. Recording it preserves what someone asserts; whether the assertion is right is for the competent proceeding, the one with authority to decide it. A case groups the claims that proceed together on one basis, such as one arbitration agreement. Jurisdiction is the authority to hear and decide the claims identified, and Recourse records the basis asserted for it and any ruling on a dispute about it. A mandate is a grant of authority to do stated things.

Intake. A centre preserves a claim and its source material before it makes any procedural decision. The claim can be taken in first, and where authority is missing the record shows it as missing while a practitioner assesses the route available.

Agreement record. For arbitration, the agreement record binds the exact bytes of the agreement, the identities of the parties, the claims covered, the rules, the administering institution, the appointment route, the seat and the governing law. The seat determines the legal system that supervises the arbitration, separately from the place of hearing. The filing refers to that record. A replacement agreement identifies its predecessor and requires reassessment of the jurisdiction and procedure findings that depended on the earlier one. Consent requires distinct signatures covering both parties and every claim in the case.

A respondent who does not take part. An existing agreement may bind a respondent who declines further participation. The competent process must determine that the agreement applies and the conditions for proceeding without renewed consent. Default has the effect prescribed by the adopted rules and the applicable law.

Referral. A referral identifies its recipient and the metadata it is permitted to carry. The parties concerned, or another competent authority, permit the disclosure; the source institution authorizes the referral; and the receiving institution separately accepts the matter. A transport receipt records delivery to an interface; the permission to disclose, the authorization of the referral and the acceptance of the matter each have their own record. Opening a receiving case requires an explicit local filing and the authority required for it. The identifiers of the source case, the referral and the receiving case remain distinct.

The court route. The competent court is the court that has jurisdiction, and its authority comes from the applicable law and from its own decisions. On this route the court decides, and an AI court prepares and supports the proceeding: it preserves the claim and the evidence, connects the claimant with a practitioner, prepares the case under that practitioner’s review as Section 3 describes, and keeps the record of each step before the court. Submission, receipt and acceptance into the court’s process are recorded as separate facts, and from then on the court controls the proceeding through its own procedures and orders.

Tracing. Mira’s practitioner can trace the stolen assets by their transaction identifiers. Tracing shows where the assets went. Evidence must separately connect the addresses, the persons who control them, Mira’s ownership and the remedy she asks for.

Court-route record. For court proceedings a practitioner records the client’s representation mandate, the intended filing and the court route identified. The record identifies the forum, the basis of jurisdiction claimed, the parties or the legally permitted descriptions of them, the relief requested and the notices required. Each action follows the requirements applicable at its procedural stage.

Service. Service means giving procedural notice through a legally effective method. A service record links the notice, the recipient, the method, the dates and the evidence of delivery to the applicable requirement. The court route also records any determination that service was effective, excused or replaced by an authorized method. A competent finding determines the procedural effect of service, and invalidated service requires reassessment of the acts that depended on it. Proceedings against an unidentified defendant, or alternative service, require a route permitted by the competent court’s law.

Interim protection. Interim protection, such as preserving assets pending a decision, requires its own authority and conditions. An urgent application can seek it where the law permits. The record retains the order, the issuing authority, the property or persons covered, the notice conditions, the expiry and the review obligations. Any action before notice requires the specific legal authority for that exception. An interim preservation order retains its own conditions and does not become a final entitlement to distribute the protected property.

Judgment. A final judgment follows the court’s procedure. Recognition elsewhere and execution against particular assets require their respective legal bases.

A.4 Engagement, preparation and evidence (Section 3)

Directory profile. A practitioner is a lawyer or other professional who acts in a proceeding. A profile states what a practitioner declares: jurisdiction, language, specialty and availability. Qualification evidence and its verification status remain explicit. A selection names the profile revision seen, because profiles change, and permits a defined private summary of the matter to be disclosed to that practitioner. If the practitioner accepts, contact is established. Finding a practitioner precedes disclosure, engagement and appointment, and a name in a directory is neither a lawyer engaged by the client nor a decision-maker independently appointed to a case.

Professional engagement. The engagement records the client, the work, the fees, the applicable duties and the clearance of conflicts. A representation mandate names the claims and actions entrusted to the practitioner, and a separate disclosure permission names what she may receive and use.

Linking record. The linking record connects the private contact matter to the case, the claims covered, the profile selected and the engagement. It binds the conflicts clearance, the representation powers and the disclosure permission. Appointment to a tribunal follows its own competent process.

Case package. Each version of a package identifies its recipient, its purpose, the revisions of its sources, its exact contents and its receipt. The analysis names its sources, separates assertions from interpretations and recommendations, and keeps contrary evidence accessible. Each cited decision retains its source and its relevance. A search that reports no result identifies the material searched and its cutoff. Changed evidence or changed authority requires reassessment before submission.

Work record of an AI agent. The record identifies the actor, the inputs, the outputs, the model and tool versions, and the permitted purpose. Authority to submit or to act comes from the delegation, and documents remain evidence even when they contain instructions: if a message in Kofi’s file says that the temperature log should be disregarded, the agent records what the message says and goes on reading the log.

Review. The responsible practitioner can inspect the underlying material and correct the analysis, and she reviews the package before using it. The review record keeps her corrections and her responsibility for what was used.

Custody. A content digest is a value computed from the bytes of a document that identifies them. Custody records link exact bytes to their declared digest algorithms. A reference identifies expected content. Receipt and verification record possession and integrity of those bytes, so an institution knows which documents it was given, by whom and on what authority. The competent process determines the weight of the evidence.

Documents issued by a court. An external court document retains the issuing authority’s own authentication or a legally sufficient certification. A protocol signature can attest receipt or verification, and it identifies that act separately from the court’s issuing of the order.

Disclosure. Each disclosure identifies its recipient, its content, its purpose and its authority. Institutions apply their own rules on privilege, retention and legal holds. Revocation can restrict future access. Bytes already disclosed remain with their recipients, subject to the recipients’ legal duties.

A.5 The decision (Sections 4 and 6)

Procedures. The arbitration design has three procedures: record resolution, expedited tribunal proceedings and full tribunal proceedings [1, §5]. An AI court acts under the first, the record procedure, which the parties adopt in their arbitration agreement: the court decides on the record of the case. Within it, record resolution in the narrow sense is the computing of findings of record, and findings of judgment go as far as the mandate goes. The procedure thus has two parts. Findings of record are made by query, which is the record resolution of the formal paper unchanged, and what the formal paper establishes about record queries applies to them. Findings of judgment are made by the models and the certifying practitioner. In Kofi’s case record resolution establishes that the service report carries the engineer’s signature and when it was recorded; whether the overhaul satisfied the contract takes further evidence and legal judgment. Where evidence is missing, the result is recorded as unresolved. Under a mandate narrower than the one in Kofi’s contract, the verdict would settle what the record answers, and the rest would go to a tribunal.

Choice of procedure. The selection of a procedure records its reasons, the notice given, the objections made and the authority under which it was adopted. A case that groups several claims must accommodate its most demanding claim or separate the claims appropriately. Referral to another centre remains a separate institutional act.

The questions. The verdict answers a list of questions. The court’s administrator draws the list from the claim’s own terms, meaning what is payable on what condition, and from each side’s case. Every point either side makes maps to a question, or to a recorded reason why it raises none. Both sides see the list, and the terms as written out, before any model runs, and may contest either; a dispute about the list is itself a question for the practitioner. The list is complete relative to the terms as written out, and a missing question is a ground of challenge.

Sources and precedent. The record of the case consists of each side’s case package and submissions, reviewed and submitted by the practitioner responsible for them, and it is closed before any model runs. The verdict identifies each precedent it relies on. Precedent in the court is as Section 6 states, and includes awards and judgments in cases it administered. Where a person displaced a finding, later cases follow her argument, and the displaced finding has no standing. Outcomes of other courts in the network have no force unless the court adopts them.

Findings and models. A finding states its conclusion, its kind, what it cites and any earlier findings it depends on; a conclusion that cites nothing in the sources is not a finding. The three kinds of finding are those of Section 4.1. The verdict states which models reached each finding.

The example’s findings. A finding of record says what a log records. Whether the room was in fact that cold is a question of judgment once a side says the log is wrong, for example because a sensor was badly set. In the example neither side does, and the condition in clause 7 is applied to what the logs record. The two findings on the temperature log are separate so that each concerns one item of the claim. The reserved matter, time to pay, carries only its default, payment at once.

The panel. The models are trained separately, which means by different makers and none on another’s output. They are given the identical record. Their number is a parameter of the court; the example uses three. The panel for a case is drawn after the record is closed and is fixed before the first run. It can be drawn from contributions that each side and the court commit to in advance and then reveal, joined with a last value. For such a draw to give no side a say over the panel, four things have to be fixed before any contribution is made: the pool, its order, the source of the last value and the identity of the draw. The last value has to come from that one source and be authenticated, and no one may be able to know or choose it until the choices to reveal are closed and cannot be taken back. The rules have to say in advance what happens when a contribution is not revealed, when the source fails and when a draw is abandoned. Nothing is added to the panel or replaced afterwards, and a model that fails stays on it as a model with no answer. New material after the draw is admitted only by a person’s decision and is answered by the same panel.

How the answers are combined. Each model answers each question of judgment alone, a stated number of times, and once more with the parties’ names exchanged and the documents in reverse order. For each answer it gives the passages it relies on, copied exactly, its authority, short reasons, and whether a reasonable decision-maker could reach the other answer on this record. A program checks that each quoted passage is in the item it names and that each authority is identified, and a run that fails either check is void for that question. A model’s answer to a question is the definite answer it gives on most of its valid runs. A tie, or too few valid runs, is no answer, and “unknown” is an answer a model may give that is never counted towards any other. A model doubts if its runs differ, if any run says the other answer is reasonable, if any run is void or missing, or if it has no definite answer. The court publishes a quorum, which is more than half of the panel as fixed, models with no answer included; in the example it is two of three. A question is agreed when every model gives the same definite answer and none doubts. It is unsettled when a quorum gives the same definite answer and some model differs, doubts or has no answer; the verdict takes the quorum’s answer and records the other reading beside it, with its passage. It is unresolved when there is no quorum, as on an even division, or when no authority of the governing law can be cited; the verdict then has no finding on that question, gives the result under each reading, and computes no sum that needs the missing answer. The three words describe the state of the panel’s answers, so a question is unresolved whether the quorum is missing because the models divide or because responses were void or missing.

Order and change. Questions are asked in the order in which they depend on one another, and each answer records the earlier answers it was asked on. Where an earlier question is unsettled or unresolved, each later question is asked in advance on every reading a model gave, which is how the verdict can show the result under each reading. Where several earlier questions are open at once and the combinations become too many, the court’s rules set a limit, and beyond it a later question is asked once the person has decided the earlier one. Two questions that depend on each other are put as one. If an earlier answer changes, because the practitioner departs from it, because an objection adds or alters a question, or because the record is amended, every finding reached on the old answer is put again before anything is signed, to the same models on the new footing or to the practitioner, and so is every sum computed from such a finding. Nothing is certified while any finding rests on an answer that has changed. If the record is amended after the draft, which only a person’s decision can allow, the same models answer every question again, and a model whose answer differs between the two records counts as doubting. A signed verdict is coherent across the dependencies that the question list records.

Decision record. The decision record identifies the models and their versions, the tool versions, exactly what each model was given, each model’s separate verdict, the combined verdict, the certificate and any argument admitted. Which models decide is thus part of each record.

Certifying practitioner. The certifying practitioner is distinct from those who represent the parties. Three records stand behind her. Accreditation says that she is competent for a class of dispute and that her professional standing has been checked against the regulator’s register. The body named in the adopted procedure accredits certifying practitioners under published criteria, covering admission to practise, professional standing, experience in the kind of dispute and professional indemnity insurance. Appointment puts her on one case: the court’s appointing authority draws her from those accredited for the class and free of conflicts. Engagement fixes her pay and her duties before any case. The role is protected (Appendix A.8): the pay is fixed before any assignment and does not vary with the outcome, with the number of verdicts considered or with whether a verdict is signed or departed from.

What she does. She receives the verdict with its supporting record, and her certificate states the checks of Section 4.2 and what she herself decided. On an agreed finding she makes the support check. An unsettled finding she decides herself, starting from the verdict: to keep it she writes a sentence, with a passage, saying why the other reading fails, and to leave it she gives a satisfactory argument. An unresolved question she decides, with reasons in the form of a finding. She decides every objection and every reserved matter. Both sides receive the draft before certification and may object within a short fixed time. No objection is refused for its form: if one lacks form she states the finding it attacks and the ground herself, and if it raises a matter outside the list she adds the question. Her work is bounded by the findings, their citations and the two sides’ objections, since the two sides know their own files and have the motive to find what is missing. Where a side filed nothing and did not answer the draft, the check for missing questions falls to her. She reads that side’s possible answers against the whole file, the determination says that it was made without that side, and it is certified only on proof that the claim and the draft were served on that side. The period for requiring a tribunal then starts only when the certified determination has been served as well.

What she answers for. The court’s rules state who may rely on the certificate, where a claim is brought, the limit, and the insurance she must carry, which covers this activity and continues after she leaves the role. Her signature covers the evidence as well as the text: if anyone later alters what a document is said to be or where it came from, the signature no longer matches and a new one is required. It binds the version of the record, the question list, the terms as written out, every finding with the answers it rests on, her own decisions and reasons, and her appointment, and a change to any of these needs a new signature.

Deciding otherwise than the verdict. A departure displaces a finding of judgment. It names the finding and gives the new answer, and it states a ground: evidence that is new, wrongly bound or wrongly read; an authority missed or misapplied; an authority outside the court’s collection, identified by its official citation, with notice to both sides; a question missing from the list; a party not heard on a point; or a matter that the law or the procedure reserves to human judgment. It quotes the record exactly wherever it relies on it, and it states the result, which must equal the recomputation. A finding of record is never displaced by argument: new evidence adds a finding of record, and the departure displaces the finding of judgment that chooses between the two. A departure also identifies the person who makes it and her capacity. Accepting it is the check of Section 5.1 applied to this action: the person is entitled to step in, the departure has the required form, and what it cites is in the record or is submitted with it. An accepted departure becomes part of the determination and can be reviewed as the findings can. A decision to give time to pay displaces the recorded default that the sum is payable at once. Its record names the rule that reserves the matter and the default, the facts considered with their place in the record, that the other side was heard, what security the other side has meanwhile, the decision itself and its reasons. A decision about time changes no finding and no sum.

Who is held to the form, and who checks. Those who may step in are the certifying practitioner before she signs, a party by a challenge (Section 5.3) or by requiring a tribunal, and a tribunal or a court seized of the matter. The form binds the certifying practitioner and the reviewer of a challenge. A party that challenges a signed determination inside the court names a finding and a ground. The form does not bind a party objecting to a draft, a party requiring a tribunal, or a tribunal or court, and an authenticated order of a competent court takes effect whatever its reasons. A party’s request for a tribunal, a proposed different decision and an order from outside the court are separate kinds of record. People check whether an argument is right: the two sides, who receive every departure that changes what a side gets before it takes effect, with a short time to object; a second practitioner, where the rule that follows reaches; the reviewer of a challenge; and a tribunal.

A second practitioner. Where a quorum of the models gave a definite answer, the certifying practitioner leaves it, and the sum now payable changes, a second accredited practitioner, appointed in the same way, countersigns before the determination issues. If the second will not, the question goes to a tribunal under the expedited procedure. A model’s doubt does not remove the second signature, which turns on a quorum’s having answered and her having left its answer. Where the models give no quorum answer, the practitioner decides the question alone, with reasons, and no second practitioner signs. That is so when the models divide, and it is also so when enough of their responses are void or missing, even if every response gave the same answer. The rule turns on the sum now payable. A departure that leaves that sum unchanged and removes a right not yet due, moves a date or changes what secures a payment is not countersigned under it.

Matters reserved to human judgment. The adopted procedure lists the matters it reserves; the procedure in Kofi’s contract reserves time to pay and the conditions on which it is given. On a reserved matter the models make no finding, the verdict states the default, here payment at once, and the certifying practitioner decides and records her reasons. A default is not a finding. If the adopted procedure lets the models list the facts that bear on a reserved matter, or advise on it, nothing they say there is part of the verdict.

Outcomes. A case ends in one of four kinds of outcome: a determination by an AI court, an award by a tribunal, a judgment or order by a court, or a settlement, which records the parties’ own agreement. Each keeps its kind in the record, with its reasons, its claims, the obligations it creates and the evidence selected in its support. A determination states the claims decided, the obligation created, the findings and the evidence relied on, and it names the model and tool versions that produced the verdict, so it can be examined afterwards.

The determination and the tribunal. A determination has the contractual effect that the applicable law permits. An award is a different instrument, issued by a tribunal through its own proceeding. The tribunal proceeds in one of two ways: expedited, in which a sole arbitrator decides on the documents, or in full [1, §5]. When a tribunal is required, what was already performed under the determination stays recorded, and its return would be a separate act. The tribunal receives the verdict, the decision record and the determination, and it may adopt the findings that no party contests after checking them. Its award is signed by its members with their reasons. The verdict is where the tribunal’s explanation starts; it shifts no burden of proof between the two sides, and no rule of the AI court binds the tribunal.

A request for a tribunal. A request states who asks and which findings are contested, and never reasons. It concerns the items of the claim it touches, which are those whose sums depend on a contested finding by the dependencies the question list records, and the determination’s effect changes for those items only. On such an item the determination ceases to bind and remains as evidence, and whatever has not been performed on it is suspended. The other items are unaffected. The formal paper applies the rule to each part of a claim separately [1, §6]. A request that contests the court’s authority over the dispute, the validity of the agreement or the integrity of the record touches every item, and so does a request that names nothing. Either side may ask. The rule applies as far as the items can be separated, by the dependencies the question list records and by the law. One signed instruction can pay a contested item and an uncontested one. In the example Asha’s instruction for US$20,000 pays two items of US$10,000; if she required a tribunal on one of them, payment of that item would be suspended and the other would stay due. The rule for an instruction that gets no answer takes precedence (Appendix A.7): nothing is sent in place of an instruction that may still execute, so payment of the uncontested item may wait. If the instruction executes in full, what it paid stays paid, and returning the contested part is a separate act.

The period. The period for requiring a tribunal runs from completed service of the certified determination, with access to the full record. It is a stated number of days in a stated time zone, ending at a stated instant. The example uses fourteen days. Requests go through channels the procedure designates, and a request submitted through one of them before the period ends is in time whenever the court’s register receives it. The period is closed only by a certificate, issued once every designated channel has delivered everything submitted in time, and a channel that cannot confirm blocks the certificate. An independent person, who is neither the certifying practitioner nor the court’s operator, decides applications to extend the period where service was not effective or a side could not act, and decides what to do when a channel stays silent. Nothing is released, and no hold is extended, merely because time has passed. These are the formal paper’s rules for a challenge period, applied to the AI court’s determination [1].

A sum not yet due. The labour on the second compressor (Section 4.3) is neither paid nor late. If Asha disputes a later log showing that cold room 2 held −18 °C for seven days, it is a new claim under the same clause, and what force the first determination’s reading of the clause has in that claim is for the adopted procedure and the governing law.

Appointment. An appointment identifies the natural person appointed, the claims covered, the appointing authority, the acceptance and the disclosures made. A tribunal consists of natural persons, each appointed by the appointing authority, the body that the arbitration agreement names for that purpose. Directory selection supplies no appointment power. Court assignments follow the court’s own law and procedures.

Learning from outcomes. Outcomes inform later preparation and operational decisions. The feedback record identifies the deciding authority, the supporting evidence, the applicable rule version and the permitted use. For each question of judgment it holds the kind of question and the term or rule it concerns, with its version; the operation and the procedure that admitted it; each model’s proposed answer, with its passages and whether it doubted; the quorum’s answer; what the person supplied, and her reasons; any later decision on it; and its status. It goes out in parts, each with its own date: the decision when it is final, each payment when it is evidenced at the recipient, and any correction when a competent act makes it. Finality, the due date and payment are three dates, and the record never says that money was paid before it was. It carries the fact of a correction. A decided dispute nominates a procedure, which an environment adopts only after its own test (Section 6). Allegations, investigations, findings and reversals remain distinct. A later ruling can require the revision of analysis that depended on an earlier one, while preserving what occurred, and further use of confidential material requires its own authority. For Mass, the relevant learning concerns fraud and wider operating practice, including the checks and consents required, the evidence that proved useful and procedural delay. An interpretation is correct when it agrees with what the competent authority decides once the question is put, and safe when acting on it passes the institution’s whole check: the authority to act is current, no ruling it rests on has been reversed, and no duty already owed is left without a way to be completed [18]. A competent outcome is evidence of the first. Shared learning carries supporting proofs, and each receiving institution checks those proofs and decides what to adopt. Each proof states the calculation or relationship it establishes and the assumptions it rests on.

A.6 Recognition, challenges and stays (Sections 5.1 and 5.3)

Recognition record. A receiver checks an outcome against keys and rules that it configured itself and against what the intended use requires. We call acceptance into a receiving institution’s record protocol recognition; it is a statement by the receiver about its own record. Judicial recognition gives an outcome legal effect under an applicable legal route; had Asha refused to pay, Kofi would have needed it. Acceptance in one forum cannot stand in for acceptance in another. A recognition record identifies the forum, the relief covered, the conditions, the time and any subsequent review.

Submitted outcomes. The acceptance of an outcome issued through the signed case process checks its signatures, case authority, evidence and procedural dependencies, and changing the evidence bound to it requires a new signature. A submitted outcome record links a claimed determination, award or settlement to documents already in case custody. It starts unverified, and an independent verifier can record an assessment linked to authority evidence and reasons. That assessment checks structure and custody; the tribunal-signature checks, and the creation and authorization of an outcome, belong to the signed case process.

Disclosure in an exchange. Disclosure must fit the recipient’s lawful need. A mechanism that requires the whole case can serve only matters that permit that disclosure. An exchange that discloses less than the whole case requires a specified account of what it proves and of the omitted dependencies that the recipient still needs.

Challenges. A challenge identifies its target, scope, grounds and requested relief. The reviewer holds the required authority and is independent of the challenged act; in an AI court the reviewer is the court’s challenge authority, a practitioner in a protected role who took no part in the determination, cleared of conflicts and acting under her own mandate. Pending challenges restrict affected claims according to the applicable rules. A challenge to record integrity requires independent examination. A side that says a document is false, as distinct from altered, raises a question of fact for a finding of judgment. A side that attacks the digest comparison itself (Section 5.3), or the custody record, attacks the basis the court works from, and that goes to a full tribunal whatever the sum [1, §5].

Later decisions. Later decisions preserve the history they affect. An upheld challenge can invalidate reliance without deleting the evidence, and a replacement outcome identifies its predecessor, so the record can reconstruct both the earlier act and its current legal treatment.

After the decision. The grants are separate, each with its holder and its end: deciding, certifying, reviewing a challenge, keeping the status and releasing what is learned. What is passed on with an outcome states, for each item of the claim, the sum determined, whether a tribunal has been required on it, any stay or correction and, later, any award that replaces it.

Stays. A stay is an order suspending specified acts for a stated period. It identifies the actions it restricts and its effective period. Suspending proceedings differs from suspending execution. A stay is ordered in the case at the institution that decided, and it stops a payment only once the sending institution has committed it to its own record: a stay committed locally before the final check prevents sending. A stay issued after an act was performed changes what is permitted from then on, and the earlier act remains recorded. An order that varies or lifts a stay identifies its predecessor. A lifting order that is itself contested cannot silently remove the restriction under review. After a stay, return, restitution or corrective performance requires a separate authorized act and evidence of its result; the return of money already paid is a claim for repayment, linked to the first.

A.7 Execution and recovery (Sections 5.2 to 5.4)

Routes. Who may carry out an outcome depends on the route. Voluntary performance rests on the instruction of the person who owes, release from custody on a mandate that covers the property and the action, and compulsory execution on competent legal authority. The act itself is performed by an execution provider: a bank, a custodian or a registry that performs an identified action on payments, assets or records under its own authority.

Execution record. The execution record identifies the obligation, the destination, the requested action and the legal basis. A monetary instruction also identifies the asset, the amount, the unit, the entitled recipient and the account mapping. The signers and conditions required follow from the route. A voluntary transfer requires the signatures of the designated executor, the actual obligor and the beneficiary.

Current authority. Before it sends, the sender establishes current authority and restrictions from the source competent to state them. The answer must give the time up to which it is complete and what it covers, because an older snapshot leaves later events unresolved.

Execution by a court. For court execution the record includes recognition where it is required and the executing authority’s determination that the requested act may proceed. The availability of assets, priority, insolvency, exemptions, sanctions and immunity can affect that determination.

Custodians. A custodian acts within its mandate or the applicable court order.

Uncertain instructions. A bank may carry out an instruction before the sender learns the result, so repeating the instruction can pay twice, while treating silence as failure corrupts both the execution and the account of what is still owed. A replacement is allowed only on evidence that everything the original has already delivered is accounted for at the recipient and that it can deliver nothing further, or when the destination itself moves whatever the original could still pay onto the replacement. A report that no payment has been seen establishes neither, since the payment may still arrive; and if the original has already paid and the report of that payment did not reach the sender, the original can pay nothing further and a replacement would still pay twice. That the original can deliver nothing further has to count money already debited and on its way, and money held at the receiving bank that could still be released to the payee. A bank that says only that it will take no more copies of the instruction has not given the evidence, and neither has a statement that the money is held. Presenting the same instruction again is distinguished from sending a new one. Presenting it again, under the same identity, is safe only where the bank recognizes that identity for its whole window and refuses any request under it once the window has ended. A new instruction is for what is outstanding at that time, which may be less than the first instruction’s amount. A sender must not automatically repeat an economic instruction, one that moves money or assets, merely because confirmation failed.

Accounting for recovery. Each credited amount must identify the effect at the entitled recipient and its allocation to an obligation. A credit is the entry in the recipient’s account at the recipient’s bank, identified by that bank’s own entry. Two entries are two credits, and one entry reported twice is one. A report that cannot be matched to an entry adds nothing and discharges nothing until it is matched. A reconciliation assigns an effect to the obligation it performs and is itself an action that needs authority. Quantity conservation requires stable effect identities and authoritative allocation journals covering the relevant claims. Repeated evidence creates no additional effect, even under a different reference from another provider. A correction preserves history while changing the amount currently supported and the obligation still outstanding; it can reduce the current support for a historical credit, leaving an explicit deficit that requires an authorized adjustment. This accounting preserves the difference between a debt’s legal treatment and the funds a recipient actually receives.

Amounts and windows. Amounts use exact units. Partial performance leaves a balance. A reversal window is a period in which the provider or the legal route still permits a reversal. The evidence for collection must cover every applicable reversal window, and missing information about a window cannot establish collection.

Completion. Conflicting evidence can invalidate a current finding of completion while preserving the historical acts. Closing a case leaves unpaid obligations and uncertain instructions visible. Relief other than money requires evidence appropriate to the act ordered, such as a corrected register or a completed delivery.

A.8 Operating an AI court (Section 4.2)

Professional work. The network needs sustained professional work with distinct responsibilities for representation, adjudication, administration and public research. Each engagement identifies its client, role, duties and authority. An institution that takes cases needs qualified practitioners whose service is committed and paid for, and a way to replace those who cannot continue.

Protected roles. Protected roles include decision-makers, appointment and challenge authorities, case administrators and people who control material case records. Their remuneration covers related payments and benefits. We adopt the funded compensation model of the formal Recourse paper [1, §10]. Before assignment, a contract fixes each protected person’s total remuneration, payment dates, service capacity and term, and the centre reserves the full cash obligation under a separate mandate. Outcomes, recovery, hours, workload, appeals and the number of assignments cannot increase the entitlement for that term. The centre reserves the obligation from its own capital before it opens and from fees it has collected afterwards, and whoever supplies the capital gets no say over any protected function.

Independence. Ownership, control, repeat appointments, future employment and referral relationships require separate conflict examination. Decisions use sourced relationships and an authorized independent process. The administering centre receives no share of a party representative’s fee and no referral fee, and when it collects on behalf of another recipient, that recipient’s entitlement is retained. Payment for a service creates no appointment right. Contracts with service companies preserve these independence requirements. Where the supplier of the court’s deciding system also supplied a system from which evidence in the case comes, the determination says so.

Public research. Bounded public research can use defined engagements or controlled bounties with objective acceptance tests. Such work creates no right of appointment and no authority over a case. Confidential preparation requires a professional engagement with its corresponding duties.

Service payments. A client pays its practitioner’s charges and the filing charges of the institution it files with through Recourse, using an appointed payment provider, under a collection mandate that names the payer, the service, each recipient’s entitlement and the permitted use of the funds. Every fee is published and fixed before the case, and the filing fee depends on the sum claimed, which is known at filing, and never on the sum determined. A service-payment record connects the accepted request, the evidence of collection, the obligations to recipients, the disbursement instructions and the receipts under a stable identifier. The provider’s collection receipt records payment against the request. Practitioner charges link to engagements, and filing charges link to the requirements of the receiving institution. Available funds can support disbursement under current mandates and service conditions. Each disbursement instruction identifies the funds used, the recipient and the obligation paid. Collection and payment to the recipient need separate evidence, for the reason given in Section 5.4: recipient-payment evidence records delivery to the practitioner or the filing institution, and failed or uncertain instructions remain open for reconciliation. Figure 5 shows the route.

Provider contract. The contract with the payment provider specifies custody, authorization, permitted deductions, refunds, reconciliation and the treatment of failed instructions. The applicable professional and client-money rules govern the arrangement.

Accounting. Client money, security, filing payments and sums owed to practitioners keep their ownership and their permitted purpose. Funds held for another person cannot serve as the institution’s unrestricted operating funds. Earned service income has separate accounting. Service receipts remain separate from evidence that an award or judgment obligation has been performed. Payments for services neither raise a protected person’s fixed entitlement nor automatically release the reserve behind it, and a transfer into that reserve requires separate authority and accounting.

Continuity. Before accepting matters, a centre needs funded administration, qualified replacement capacity and a lawful custody-transfer route, and its successor needs current authority for every future act. Restoring a database cannot resolve whether a destination already received or performed an omitted instruction: had Kofi’s centre closed while the instruction to Asha’s bank was unconfirmed, a restored database could not have settled whether the bank had paid. A transfer preserves the case history, the unfinished work, the disclosure duties, the outstanding obligations and the reserved funds, together with original identities and uncertain instructions. Existing exposure constrains new instructions: a successor must account for performance already completed and for performance still possible under an outstanding instruction. A transfer of administration neither discharges the obligation nor supplies new capacity to pay it. The same rule applies to each role this paper adds: the certifying practitioner, the second practitioner, the reviewer of challenges, the independent person who decides extensions and the registry that closes the period. Each has a named successor or pool, funded before the case is accepted, with current authority. If the practitioner becomes unavailable before she signs, the case is reassigned in the same way and the checks are done afresh. If a reviewer is missing when a hold reaches its end, the successor decides, and nothing is released or extended by default.

A.9 What the guarantee rests on (Section 8)

The induction. Under authenticated signing identities, correct authority records, durable local transactions and outside evidence that covers the events relied on, admitted records keep the authority, evidence and earlier steps they depended on. The inductive step is that a record in which every earlier action identifies its predecessor and passed its checks still has that property after the next admitted action. Anyone given the history can confirm by replaying it that no action rests on a missing or altered predecessor.

The record and outside evidence. A disclosed history establishes what it states and covers, and later events need evidence from an appropriate source. Custody of keys, records of revocation, independently configured authority records and review guard against a compromised key or a dishonest provider supplying evidence that appears valid, where the evidence covers the event and action is taken in time. The truth of a source and the legal validity of an act stay with the authorities competent to judge them, and performance is shown by evidence from the institutions that carry it out.

The formal result. The formal Recourse paper calls its result historical integrity, and the theorem has its own model and hypotheses [1, §§9.6–9.7]. Its assumptions concern authentication, identity, ordering, economic evidence and allocation journals, the authoritative records of how identified quantities are reserved or applied across claims; the legal conditions remain external. In that model only an object signed by the natural persons of an accepted tribunal enters the set of awards, so a determination does not, whoever countersigns it.

Recovery. Legal entitlement, available property, authority to execute and performance by a provider are distinct premises. The formal result gives no positive lower bound on recovery from a qualifying award alone, and the same obstruction applies to a court judgment whenever the assets or the conditions of execution needed for collection are absent. The design secures the accounting of whatever is recovered: given stable identities for effects and authoritative allocation journals covering the relevant claims, no effect is counted twice, and a payment attributed to one claim cannot silently become available for another.

Progress and order. Progress depends on responsive authorities, practitioners, funding and evidence from the institutions instructed. Each institution puts the actions it admits into a single order, and independent institutions keep their own orders and acceptance decisions.

Appendix B. Related work in detail

Arbitral awards. Arbitration supplies a contractual route to decisions that can be recognized across borders. The 1958 Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards governs qualifying agreements and awards. Its conditions retain notice, scope, procedure, review and forum-specific limits [2, Articles II–VI]. Recourse records the evidence relevant to successive actions under those conditions.

Court judgments. Court judgments are recognized by a distinct route, which the 2019 Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Judgments in Civil or Commercial Matters supplies between contracting states. It has subject-matter exclusions, eligibility requirements and refusal grounds [3, Articles 1–7 and 13]. Interim measures fall outside its definition of a judgment, and it supplies conditions for recognition without supplying general jurisdiction to hear a claim. The court route therefore identifies the applicable domestic or treaty route for each requested act. A competent court decides recognition and execution, and separate routes for arbitration and for courts preserve the legal basis of each proceeding.

Procedure. The 2016 Technical Notes on Online Dispute Resolution give non-binding guidance on fairness, confidentiality, due process, transparency and accountability [4], and the 2021 Expedited Arbitration Rules provide simplified procedures through express party agreement [5, Article 1]. We inherit consent and expedited procedure as established mechanisms. Our specification connects the concerns of the Technical Notes to the passage from private contact to authorized professional work. Our contribution binds the record procedure and the expedited procedure to engagement, prepared evidence and payment records, and specifies the interfaces and the authority they require.

Tools in use. The tools already in use stop at authentication. Case-management systems, practitioner directories, electronic signatures and electronic filing preserve, identify, authenticate and deliver; none establishes who may represent a party, which institution may decide or who may act on the decision.

Decision by machine. Disputes are already decided by machine in two settings: online marketplaces determine their own customers’ disputes in very large numbers under their own terms, and an arbitral institution has offered an AI arbitrator for two-party, documents-only disputes, in which a human arbitrator reviews the machine’s draft award [11]. In Recourse the parties choose the court by agreement and can require a tribunal, and the verdict is built as findings that cite the record. The use of AI to decide disputes, by agreement in arbitration and under legislation in the courts, has been examined in public lectures by Sir Geoffrey Vos as Master of the Rolls [11–17].

Digital assets. Under the Digital Dispute Resolution Rules of 2021 a tribunal can operate relevant digital assets through signing or other control mechanisms available to it [6, Rule 11]. In Recourse each independent provider acts under its own mandate. That separation requires exact instructions and subsequent evidence of effect.

Histories. Retained histories and conditional transitions are established techniques, and research on temporal databases distinguishes when a fact applies from when a database records it. We use them to preserve case and payment events with their authority and evidence [7, §4].

Accession. Accession Networks analyses when common accession terms can reproduce relationships otherwise formed through bilateral negotiation. Its result depends on the common terms and on accurate treatment of participation and exceptions, and the same conditions apply when centres adopt common participation arrangements [8].

Recognition. The Recognition Protocol separates technical conformance from competent recognition. We preserve that distinction when a receiving institution checks an outcome: the institution’s own authority and legal basis govern its reliance [9].

Formal research. The formal Recourse research supplies the record procedure and unconditional tribunal access [1, §§5–6], with the model that separates authority from effect, the tiers of procedure and the independence requirements used here [1, §§5–12]. It develops professional supply, protected remuneration, review and continuity, and Allocated Title provides related analysis of legal rights, custody records and provider performance. We use those results under their stated assumptions [1, 10].

Learning. The institutional world model through which a Mass environment learns, and the conditions under which institutions share what they learn, are developed in a companion paper [18].
